# DESS Standard Minutes Profile

This document holds the complete standards and operating logic for drafting the minutes. Part A, the Professional Standards, sets what the minutes must achieve: nine Minuting Policy dimensions (MP1 to MP9: what to record and in what depth) and six Writing Style dimensions (WS1 to WS6: how to write and present it). Part B, the Operational AI Logic, sets how to apply those standards to the meeting evidence, stage by stage. Apply both in full.

# Part A: Professional Standards

## DESS Standard Minutes Profile — Professional Standards

Version 1.0  |  9 November 2026

### 1 Executive summary

This document establishes the DESS Standard Minutes Profile for UK board and board committee minutes across nine Minuting Policy dimensions and six Writing Style dimensions. It defines a complete professional standard for preparing and refining high-quality minutes and provides the foundation for translating that standard into operational AI logic. Its research foundation is CGI/ICSA Minute Taking, April 2017, and the expanded October 2026 research on UK minute-taking requirements and professional practice.

DESS provides one universal Standard Minutes Profile, suitable for immediate use by UK organisations without sector-specific configuration. The standard draws on the full legal, regulatory and professional research foundation to establish sound minute-taking generally. Particular sector, organisational, board and committee requirements are addressed through the Customised Minutes Profile. The universal baseline does not, by itself, establish compliance with every organisation’s applicable obligations; those obligations cannot be overridden by preference.

The standard is a faithful, substantive and proportionate account of business, outcomes, principal reasoning, material challenge and agreed follow-up. It should enable an informed future reader to understand what happened and why, without reconstructing the discussion. The depth of the record reflects significance and complexity, rather than the volume of speech. Routine matters can be concise; material judgements, risks and competing considerations need fuller explanation.

Minuting Policy determines what belongs in the record. Writing Style governs how that content is expressed and presented. Ordinary deliberation is narrated collectively, with individuals identified where their identity is material. Decisions, actions, recommendations and delegations remain distinct. Unsupported compliance recitals, invented explanations and retrospective improvement of the meeting are excluded. No single transcript, agenda, paper or note is automatically definitive in every circumstance.

The standards apply to preparation and refinement: the initial AI draft, professional editing normally in Word, further drafts incorporating circulation and review comments, and a refined version ready to be put forward for approval. Formal submission, approval itself and subsequent management of the official record are outside this standard. The standards govern minuting intelligence, not the workflow or status management surrounding it. Recording a procedural event that occurred in a meeting remains within scope.

### 2 Research foundation scope and methodology

#### 2.1 Evidence base

The research foundation comprises CGI/ICSA Minute Taking, April 2017 [S1], and the expanded October 2026 report UK Board and Committee Minute-Taking Requirements and Professional Practice [S2]. The expanded report synthesises relevant legal, regulatory and professional sources; it is not itself legislation or regulatory guidance. Together these sources support the professional principles, evidential distinctions and reasoned judgements set out in this publication.

#### 2.2 Methodology principles

Evidence-led: derive standards from relevant legal and regulatory requirements, CGI guidance and the expanded research. Identify the authority and scope of the underlying source rather than treating every cited proposition as equally binding.

Professional judgement: where sources leave legitimate choices, select and explain a high-quality default. The aim is not to average practice or imitate an individual governance professional. A chosen convention can be sound even when another convention is equally acceptable.

Defensible and adaptable: maintain a clear rationale for every default and permit legitimate organisational or committee customisation. Customisation must preserve accuracy, material meaning and applicable obligations. Historical minutes show practice; repetition alone does not establish legal necessity or professional superiority.

#### 2.3 Authority and applicability

| Category | Treatment within the professional standard |
| --- | --- |
| Legal requirement | An applicable statute or legal instrument is binding within its scope. A duty concerning governance conduct or records does not automatically prescribe the wording of minutes. |
| Regulatory requirement or expectation | Distinguish binding rules, comply-or-explain Code provisions, regulatory guidance and supervisory expectations. Preserve their actual scope and authority. |
| Established professional guidance | CGI is the principal general minute-taking source. FRC supporting guidance informs good practice and is distinct from the Code itself. |
| Reasoned professional judgement | DESS selects clear defaults where the sources allow alternatives, including the depth of reasons, purposeful attribution and expression conventions. |
| Organisational or individual preference | House conventions may legitimately vary. Governing documents, committee powers and adopted policies may also create particular requirements, rather than mere preferences. |

#### 2.4 One universal professional baseline

There is one DESS Standard Minutes Profile across all 15 dimensions. Use of it does not require sector selection or organisational configuration, and it does not activate different defaults for different bodies. Sector research contributes evidence of sound professional practice to the same baseline. Differences in particular legal, regulatory, sector, organisational and committee requirements belong in the Customised Minutes Profile, whose design is outside this document.

This distinction permits immediate use of a defensible professional standard without asserting universal legal compliance. Particular obligations remain applicable regardless of whether an organisation chooses to customise. A preference cannot override them. The customisation discussion in each dimension identifies legitimate differences; it does not define alternative DESS defaults or prerequisites for selecting the standard.

#### 2.5 Scope of professional intelligence

The standard covers UK board and board committee minutes prepared and refined from meeting materials, including transcripts, agendas, papers and notes. It supports the first draft, professional editing and further refinement following circulation and review. It does not specify how those stages operate or how successive versions are managed. The end point for this standard is a refined account ready to be put forward for approval.

Formal submission for approval, approval procedures, access permissions and retention management are outside the profile. Their exclusion does not remove genuine content requirements. If a meeting approves earlier minutes, considers a disclosure request or makes a records-related decision, its actual substantive business and procedural events should be recorded in the normal way. Recording that event is different from administering the process it concerns.

Legal privilege remains in scope because it can affect what the minutes should contain. MP9 requires careful treatment and appropriate professional legal judgement. The standard neither assumes that the AI can independently decide privilege nor makes a label sufficient to establish protection. No prompts, algorithms, source-ranking rules, clarification workflow, system architecture or customisation interface are prescribed.

#### 2.6 Legal and evidential context

Companies Act section 248 requires minutes of directors’ proceedings and a minimum retention period; section 249 gives authenticated minutes evidential significance and rebuttable presumptions. These provisions explain why accuracy matters. Retention and authentication administration are not translated into requirements for the minute-writing intelligence. Section 248 should not be presented as a standalone statutory regime for every committee [S3; S2, pp6–7].

Directors’ duties in sections 171–177 concern conduct, and section 182 may be relevant to declarations. They do not prescribe a universal recital in every minute. Actual powers, constitutional provisions and terms of reference explain why a decision, recommendation or delegation must be described accurately [S3–S4]. A professional standard cannot make an invalid procedure valid by describing it as compliant.

The UK Corporate Governance Code operates within its applicable Listing Rules and comply-or-explain framework; its supporting FRC guidance is separate. Charity Commission materials cited here concern England and Wales, rather than every UK charity regime. Pension and public-body materials illustrate other particular obligations [S5–S13]. These distinctions inform the evidence base and the scope for customisation, without creating sector variants of the DESS standard.

#### 2.7 Source verification and provenance

Targeted source verification on 9 November 2026 covered Companies Act sections 248–249, the FRC decision-recording passage and Code scope, Charity Commission meeting guidance and the pension-meeting record provision. The FRC guidance page was updated on 4 November 2026; paragraph 31 addresses significant decisions. CC48 recommends recording who voted and how, a particular charity guidance expectation rather than a universal corporate voting rule. The pension meeting-record provision is regulation 13 [S3, S5–S7, S11].

Section 7 distinguishes primary materials checked directly from sources used through the expanded research report. The report’s broad local-government references establish that particular regimes exist, but are not a complete current legal specification for every council. The research foundation supports a universal professional standard without claiming exhaustive verification of every sector obligation.

#### 2.8 Coherence of the fifteen dimensions

MP1 establishes meeting and business context; MP2 identifies the evidential basis; MP3 determines substantive depth across the whole record. MP4 distinguishes outcomes, MP5 explains reasons, MP6 preserves scrutiny, MP7 captures follow-up and authority, and MP8 records material procedure. MP9 protects accuracy and the boundary between the meeting and later events. An issue may affect several dimensions without being recorded repeatedly.

WS1 sets narrative form, WS2 organises the selected content, WS3 makes it clear and concise, WS4 expresses participant identities, WS5 makes outcomes and actions explicit, and WS6 maintains consistent document presentation. WS2 concerns logical organisation; WS6 concerns headings, numbering, references and formatting. WS6 does not determine document status or version management.

The substantive judgement precedes its expression. MP6 and MP8 establish when identity is material; WS4 determines how to refer to the person. MP4 and MP7 establish commitments and powers; WS5 must preserve their force. Grammar, layout and brevity cannot remove material reasons, qualifications or dissent, enlarge delegated authority or turn uncertainty into certainty.

#### 2.9 Accuracy customisation and the professional floor

The universal default is professionally defensible in its own right. A governance professional’s preferred convention is not presumed superior, and the standard does not require personalisation to become usable. Customisation may reflect legitimate organisational requirements, different acceptable conventions or a justified depth of treatment. It cannot justify invention, misleading omission or breach of applicable obligations.

Available evidence may be incomplete or contradictory. Produce the most accurate account it supports, preserve material uncertainty and obtain professional clarification where needed. Minor discrepancies that do not change substantive accuracy should receive proportionate attention. The standard does not require one source to prevail automatically or allow a better decision process to be substituted for the one that occurred.

Each dimension contains its purpose, Default Professional Standard, supporting evidence, professional rationale, scope for customisation and application of the standard. The application guidance explains how to interpret the professional principles in practice. Section 5 reproduces all 15 Default Professional Standards verbatim.

### 3 Nine Minuting Policy dimensions

#### MP1 Meeting and business context

##### A Purpose

Establish the identity, capacity and circumstances of the meeting, and the business to which each minute relates. Context makes the record intelligible and helps distinguish the acts of one legal entity or governing body from another.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Identify the organisation and board or committee, meeting date, time, venue and means of participation, chair, minute taker, members present, others in attendance and apologies. Record material changes in attendance against the affected business. Identify each substantive item and provide enough background to explain its purpose, stage and relationship to previous decisions. Distinguish each entity’s business and the capacity in which participants acted. Include additional location, timing or procedural context where it matters to authority, participation or applicable requirements. Do not invent missing particulars or repeat background that adds no understanding.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 3 and 5.1, printed pp7–8 and 11–12, support meeting particulars and a comprehensible record; section 5.3.7, pp25–27, addresses subsidiaries and transactional meetings. Legal context: Companies Act section 248 concerns directors’ proceedings, but does not prescribe this complete list [S3]. Sector context: prescribed pension-trustee meeting particulars are identified in the research [S2, pp9, 26; S11]. Professional judgement: the complete baseline and explicit separation of entity and capacity are DESS choices derived from those sources.

##### D Professional rationale

A future reader should be able to establish which body met and understand the business without reconstructing a transcript. A subsidiary’s record must reflect its own deliberation and decision; group context does not replace that responsibility. Attendance changes need item-level precision where eligibility or quorum is affected, rather than a minute-by-minute attendance diary.

##### E Scope for customisation

Additional company numbers, remote participant locations, time zones, invited absentees, public and private sessions or next-meeting particulars may be established through a Customised Minutes Profile where useful or required. Particular pension, charity or public-body obligations illustrate why additional particulars may be necessary; they do not create different DESS defaults. House templates may arrange particulars differently. Omitting the entity, relevant attendance or material capacity distinctions would weaken the standard.

##### F Application of the standard

Record attendance changes by affected item as the general convention, using exact times where they matter or are required. The same universal professional standard applies across organisations. Any particular requirements about additional meeting particulars belong in the Customised Minutes Profile.

#### MP2 Information and evidential basis

##### A Purpose

Govern what the record says about papers, reports, advice and other information used in the meeting. Preserve the difference between information available, information considered and conclusions actually reached.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Identify material papers and advice actually received or considered, using a clear title or reference and version or date where necessary. Summarise the evidence, assumptions, limitations and corrections that materially affected deliberation or outcome. Distinguish a presenter’s report or assertion from the body’s own conclusion. Refer to supporting papers without treating all their contents as adopted or reproducing them unnecessarily. Preserve material uncertainty and discrepancies; seek clarification of consequential gaps rather than supply an evidential basis that the meeting did not establish.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI section 5.3.2, pp17–18, distinguishes papers from minutes; section 5.3.8, pp30–31, supports identifying papers and their circulation. Section 6.1, pp32–33, recognises contemporaneous notes and technical review. Sector regulatory guidance: CC48 and CC27 emphasise underlying information and advice for charities in England and Wales [S7–S8; S2, pp8–10]. Professional judgement: the explicit treatment of conflicting sources and unsupported assertions extends the accuracy principle to first-draft preparation; it is not a statutory source-ranking rule.

##### D Professional rationale

A paper shows what was put forward, not necessarily what the board accepted. Conversely, a transcript may omit non-verbal proceedings or misidentify a speaker. Reliable minutes reconcile the available evidence through professional review; they do not automatically privilege one input in every case. Material evidence should be traceable while the minute remains useful without reprinting the pack. Minor discrepancies that do not affect substantive accuracy need proportionate treatment; they need not turn every drafting task into an unresolved evidential dispute.

##### E Scope for customisation

Transactional decisions may identify an approved document precisely or incorporate a specified annex. Audit or risk committees may need fuller records of assumptions, assurance and specialist advice. A shorter reference can suffice for routine reports. It is unacceptable to convert a recommendation in a paper into an approval, or silently resolve a material contradiction for the sake of completeness.

##### F Application of the standard

No source has automatic precedence in every circumstance. Consequential discrepancies require professional clarification. Minor discrepancies that do not affect substantive accuracy should be treated proportionately; they should not distract from producing the most accurate account supported by the evidence.

#### MP3 Materiality and proportionality

##### A Purpose

Determine the depth of the record across all other policy dimensions. Materiality concerns significance to understanding proceedings, decisions, oversight and accountability, rather than the amount of speech or the length of a paper.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Record every substantive item of business and its disposition, with detail proportionate to its significance. Give fuller treatment to material decisions, difficult judgements, significant risks, competing options, important stakeholder effects, substantive challenge and unusual procedure. Keep routine administrative and noting items brief when their context and outcome are clear. Exclude repetition, incidental exchanges and operational detail without governance value. Retain material exceptions, unresolved concerns and mandatory particulars even where the resulting record is longer. Do not use uniform length, financial thresholds or a decision-only summary as the default measure of quality.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 3 and 5.3.2, pp7–8 and 16–18, support sufficient but proportionate detail. Sector regulatory guidance: CC27’s decision-recording discussion supports significance-based detail [S8; S2, pp8–12]. Legal boundary: section 248 requires a record of directors’ proceedings; it does not establish a word count or exempt substantive non-decision business [S3]. Professional judgement: the significance factors and preservation of concise dispositions for routine business are DESS’s synthesis, not a numerical legal test.

##### D Professional rationale

Good minutes allocate attention according to what matters. A long noting paper may require a short minute, while a short but contested approval may need substantial reasoning. Oversight without a decision can itself be material. Detail is sufficient when a future reader can understand the significant business and accountability without being misled by compression or overwhelmed by dialogue.

##### E Scope for customisation

Bodies may adopt fuller narrative, compact records or additional sector-specific detail. No single monetary threshold is suitable across organisations. Small or non-trading subsidiaries may have straightforward business, but subsidiary status alone does not justify reduced quality. A blanket instruction to omit reasons or challenge, or record only resolutions, would compromise the professional baseline.

##### F Application of the standard

The default is a substantive, proportionate narrative. Neither maximal detail nor bare decisions is the general measure of quality. There are no fixed word counts or numerical materiality thresholds.

#### MP4 Outcomes and decisions

##### A Purpose

Identify what the body actually concluded and the authority and limits of that conclusion. Prevent discussion, endorsement, noting and formal approval from being treated as interchangeable.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Record the actual disposition of each substantive item: decision, approval, recommendation, referral, rejection, deferral, receipt or noting, or discussion without a concluded decision. Preserve the scope, material terms, conditions, limits and effective timing of an outcome where established. Distinguish a committee’s exercise of delegated authority from its recommendation to another body. Record material voting results, abstentions and formal dissent where relevant or required. Do not infer approval from silence, a favourable discussion or a paper’s proposed resolution. Where the outcome is materially unclear, identify the need for confirmation rather than manufacture certainty.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 3, 4.1 and 5.3.2, pp7–8, 10 and 17–18, support clear decisions and clarification. Sections 5.3.3–5.3.4 address abstention and dissent. Constitutional context: actual articles, reserved matters and terms of reference determine authority; Model Articles are examples only [S4; S2, pp6–7, 16]. Sector variation: CC48 includes voting records and exact voted resolutions [S7]. Professional judgement: the outcome categories and default treatment of uncertainty organise these principles without creating a new legal taxonomy.

##### D Professional rationale

An intelligible record must tell the reader what was authorised, what remained open and who still needed to decide. A committee can validly approve matters within delegated powers; it should not automatically be confined to recommendation language. Equally, “approved” would misstate a recommendation-only committee’s act. Conditions are integral to the decision rather than optional background.

##### E Scope for customisation

Formal voting, named votes, prescribed resolutions and transactional wording may be required. Corporate consensus decisions need not acquire an invented roll-call vote. Organisations may use different labels if the substantive distinction remains clear. Never imply unanimity merely because no objection was captured, or improve an unclear conclusion into an unconditional approval.

##### F Application of the standard

Record the actual collective outcome without calling it unanimous unless supported. Preserve distinctions between decision, recommendation, action and delegation. Uncertainty about a material outcome or term requires professional clarification, rather than an assumed resolution.

#### MP5 Decision reasoning and relevant considerations

##### A Purpose

Govern the explanation of why a decision was reached and which considerations materially influenced it. Distinguish the body’s reasoning from the draft writer’s assessment of what it ought to have considered.

##### B Default Professional Standard

For substantive decisions, record the principal reasons and enough context to understand the judgement reached. Include material options, arguments, risks, benefits, assumptions, advice and stakeholder or beneficiary effects actually considered, where they explain the outcome. Preserve significant trade-offs, conditions and unresolved limitations. A routine decision may have an evident rationale needing only a short explanation or clear contextual reference. Do not invent reasons, reconstruct a better decision process, or insert generic assurances that statutory duties or every possible alternative were considered. Where material reasoning is not established, identify the gap for professional review.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI section 5.3.2, p17, supports reasons and sufficient context; sections 3 and 5.3.7 address duties and transactional rationale. FRC guidance paragraph 31 supports recording issues and reasons for significant decisions [S6]. Legal context: Companies Act sections 171–177 govern conduct, not a universal recital in minutes [S3]. Charity guidance is more explicit on reasons and relevant factors [S8; S2, pp8–10]. Professional judgement: a presumption of a stated rationale for substantive decisions is the DESS standard.

##### D Professional rationale

The lasting value of a decision record often lies in its explanation. The default deliberately goes beyond bare outcomes without requiring an essay for every approval. Reasons can be faithfully synthesised from the deliberation; they need not have been announced in a single concluding sentence. Synthesis must not supply a rationale that the available meeting evidence cannot support.

##### E Scope for customisation

Charity decisions may need fuller explanations; regulated, contentious or high-risk matters warrant greater detail. Routine implementation can refer clearly to a previous decision and explain the current step. Applicable creditor-interest duties or other legal circumstances may make different considerations material; specialist judgement is needed. A style preference cannot remove a material reason or falsely assert compliance.

##### F Application of the standard

Record principal reasons for substantive decisions, with concise treatment where routine rationale is evident. Reasons can be synthesised faithfully from the available evidence but cannot be invented. There is no standard section 172 compliance paragraph.

#### MP6 Challenge and oversight

##### A Purpose

Capture meaningful scrutiny, assurance and unresolved concerns, including oversight business that does not culminate in a resolution. Preserve the substance and effect of challenge without turning the record into a performance assessment of speakers.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Record material questions, concerns and challenges, the significant response or assurance given, and their effect on the outcome or further work. Preserve unresolved issues, requests for further evidence, limitations of assurance and any agreed escalation. Record formal dissent and a request for dissent to be recorded; include the material basis where established. Preserve significant unresolved director concerns even without a final decision or a specific request for attribution. Do not claim that scrutiny was robust, satisfactory or unanimous without an evidential basis, and do not record every exchange merely to demonstrate participation.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 3, 5.3.2 and 5.3.4–5.3.5, pp7–8, 17 and 21–23, support challenge and dissent. Code status: Provision 8 addresses unresolved director concerns for companies within its scope, through the comply-or-explain framework [S5]. FCA guidance and PRA supervisory expectations give heightened relevance to oversight [S9–S10; S2, pp8, 14–15]. Professional judgement: retaining material unresolved concerns across the general DESS baseline extends a sound governance principle beyond bodies formally subject to that Code.

##### D Professional rationale

“The board challenged management” says little unless the reader can understand the issue tested and what followed. A neutral summary should retain both challenge and material response, including an unsatisfactory or absent answer. The absence of recorded challenge cannot be repaired by inventing it. Earlier views modified through discussion are different from dissent that remained at the conclusion.

##### E Scope for customisation

More individual attribution is appropriate where accountability, governing rules or significant personal positions require it. Charity guidance expects voting detail; public bodies may have additional requirements. Fuller technical scrutiny may be needed for audit and risk committees. Neither collective narration nor an individual’s preference justifies suppressing a material unresolved concern, dissent or adverse assurance.

##### F Application of the standard

Ordinary challenge is narrated collectively. Identify formal dissenters and individuals whose significant continuing concern or accountability requires personal identification, with its material basis where established. Preserve the distinction between an issue tested in deliberation and a concern remaining unresolved.

#### MP7 Actions and delegation

##### A Purpose

Identify substantive follow-up and distinguish responsibility to perform work from authority to make further decisions. Preserve accountability without filling minutes with incidental management instructions.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Record agreed board or committee actions, the required work or deliverable, the responsible person or body, and the deadline or reporting point where agreed. Include material conditions, dependencies and reporting obligations. Record delegations distinctly, identifying the recipient, the authority granted and its scope, limits and conditions as established. Distinguish agreed action from suggestion, request for consideration or an offer of assistance. Do not invent owners, dates or delegated powers. A separate action schedule may supplement the minutes but must not replace the substantive record of the commitment or authority created.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 3 and 5.3.2, pp7 and 17–18, support actions, agreed deadlines and delegated authority, with materiality applied to follow-up. Constitutional/legal context: Model Articles illustrate delegation provisions; actual powers depend on the organisation’s constitution and governance instruments [S4]. Research: S2, pp10 and 16, distinguishes delegation and reserved matters. Professional judgement: expressing a deliverable and separating implementation responsibility from decision authority are DESS choices supporting the underlying guidance.

##### D Professional rationale

A useful record enables a reader to understand both the commitment and who is accountable. An action to prepare a proposal does not authorise its implementation. A delegation to finalise terms must not lose any cap, consultation requirement or obligation to return to the board. Missing particulars are a legitimate clarification issue, rather than grounds for creating plausible obligations.

##### E Scope for customisation

Organisations may group actions at item endings or in a table, use roles or names, and maintain an action register. Committees may require more detailed reporting or escalation. Low-level operational suggestions may stay outside the minutes unless materially relevant. A preferred format cannot remove limits on authority, disguise an unassigned action or convert an indicative target into an agreed deadline.

##### F Application of the standard

Retain an established action even when a material owner, deadline or condition is incomplete, and identify the missing information for professional review. A delegation must retain its actual scope and limitations. Do not create missing particulars or confuse implementation work with decision-making authority.

#### MP8 Governance and procedure

##### A Purpose

Record the procedural facts needed to understand participation, decision authority and the handling of conflicts. Avoid presenting formal wording as evidence that a procedure actually happened.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Record material declarations of interest, identify the person and nature of the interest sufficiently, and state the actual management of the conflict, including withdrawal, non-participation, authorisation or other measures where applicable. Capture quorum issues, material attendance changes, voting procedure, notice objections, adjournments and other procedural events affecting the business. Record relevant chair confirmations when made, and particulars required by applicable rules. Do not assume quorum, due notice, absence of conflicts or procedural compliance from a standard template. Distinguish discussion held without decision-making authority from a validly taken decision; identify unresolved procedural uncertainty for review.

##### C Supporting evidence

Legal context: Companies Act sections 175, 177 and 182 address conflicts and declarations in their respective circumstances; the articles affect participation and quorum [S3–S4]. These duties are not a universal minute wording prescription. Professional guidance: CGI sections 5.2–5.2.3, pp12–15, and 5.3.6, pp24–25, address actual procedural facts. Organisational requirements may be binding internally. Professional judgement: the fact-based baseline adopts CGI’s selective approach while preserving circumstances in which explicit confirmations are useful or required.

##### D Professional rationale

Procedure matters where it affects whose decision was taken and whether participants could properly act. A recital of compliance is weaker than a reliable account of an actual conflict and its treatment. An attendance list may evidence an ordinary quorum, but a withdrawal or connectivity failure can change that position for a particular decision. The minute should report facts without purporting to give an unsupported legal validity opinion.

##### E Scope for customisation

Charities, pensions, public bodies and transactional meetings may need fuller procedural records, named votes or formal confirmations. Proposers and seconders can be included where required or useful. An organisation may favour a brief opening quorum statement when supported. Customisation cannot assert a procedure that did not occur or omit a material challenge to validity.

##### F Application of the standard

The default uses actual procedural facts and supported confirmations. It does not automatically assert absence of conflicts, due notice, quorum or compliance. Preserve material procedural events, including events relating to approval of earlier minutes where they actually form part of the meeting business; this is recording proceedings, not managing approval.

#### MP9 Boundaries and integrity of the record

##### A Purpose

Protect the minute as a faithful record of the meeting, including its temporal limits. Govern exclusions, sensitive content and uncertainty without suppressing significant business.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Record the meeting that occurred, excluding irrelevant conversation, repetition, gratuitous personal detail and unsupported inference. Preserve material disagreement, adverse information and uncertainty. Do not omit significant business merely because a participant says it is off the record. Keep subsequent developments distinct from the proceedings; any necessary post-meeting note must be exceptional, clearly identified and dated. For potentially privileged advice, ordinarily record that advice was received and the relevant business outcome, with appropriate professional legal judgement governing substantive treatment. Do not assume that the AI can independently determine legal privilege. Include sensitive personal or commercial detail only to the extent needed for a faithful and sufficient account. Do not silently sanitise, retrospectively improve or fabricate the proceedings.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 4, 5.3.8 and 6.1–6.2, pp9–10, 27–31 and 32–34, support accuracy, careful privilege treatment and resistance to rewriting history. CGI describes competing post-meeting practices, rather than one mandated solution. Legal context: section 249 explains the evidential importance of an accurate record [S3]. S2, pp28–29, identifies the contextual judgement needed for privilege. Professional judgement: the boundary on off-record requests, exceptional use of later notes and explicit limit on independent AI determination of privilege are DESS standards derived from record integrity.

##### D Professional rationale

Minutes need not reproduce every remark, but exclusion must follow relevance and applicable requirements rather than reputational convenience. Draft review may correct a mistaken account of the meeting; it must not turn later knowledge into earlier consideration. Legal advice can sometimes be minuted without losing privilege, as CGI explains. Merely labelling text privileged does not establish protection, and business decisions are not automatically privileged.

##### E Scope for customisation

Legally advised segregated annexes or fuller records of privileged discussion may be appropriate where the organisation’s circumstances justify them. An organisation may prohibit post-meeting notes altogether. Any variation belongs in the Customised Minutes Profile and must preserve a faithful and sufficient account. Sensitive content that needs to be recorded should be treated carefully, rather than removed solely because it is sensitive. Determining the content is within this dimension; administering access, approval or retention is not.

##### F Application of the standard

Post-meeting notes are exceptional, separately identified and dated where necessary. Ordinarily record receipt of potentially privileged advice and the relevant business outcome. Appropriate professional legal judgement governs substantive treatment; the AI is not assumed able to determine privilege independently.

### 4 Six Writing Style dimensions

#### WS1 Voice tense and tone

##### A Purpose

Govern the narrative form used to express selected content. Maintain a neutral organisational account while preserving responsibility, certainty and the timing of commitments.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Use formal, plain British English in an objective third-person narrative, normally in reported speech and the past tense for proceedings. Describe prospective actions from the meeting’s perspective without weakening a firm commitment. Use the active voice where it makes responsibility clearer and the passive voice where the collective outcome is the focus. Preserve uncertainty, qualifications and the distinction between an assertion and an established conclusion. Avoid promotional, emotive or evaluative language, conversational dialogue and unsupported descriptions of the quality of governance. Retain exact operative wording where necessary, even when it uses a different grammatical form.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 5.3–5.3.1, pp15–16, recommend reported speech, past tense and conventional future-in-the-past wording. Section 5.3.5 cautions against drafting for regulatory appearance. These are conventions, not legal obligations. Research: S2, pp12–14, recognises contemporary alternatives. Professional judgement: British English, selective active voice and priority for an unambiguous commitment are DESS expression choices; they adapt the convention without changing the substantive record.

##### D Professional rationale

Reported speech creates a stable account separated from dialogue. Mechanical substitution of “should” for an agreed obligation can change its force; conditional grammar must not make a firm action appear optional. Equally, a forecast should not become a promise. Tone should remain calm even when the record describes serious disagreement or failed assurance.

##### E Scope for customisation

Contemporary narrative, limited present tense for continuing facts or a different language may be acceptable. Accessible wording can suit public records. Direct wording may be required for a resolution. Variation must preserve timing, certainty, decision terms and the distinction between fact and reported information. Neither informality nor traditional formality is inherently a higher professional standard.

##### F Application of the standard

Reported speech and past tense are the professional baseline, with faithful meaning taking priority over rigid grammar. An agreed commitment must retain its force; a forecast must not become a promise. Alternative writing conventions may be equally professionally acceptable.

#### WS2 Structure and sequence

##### A Purpose

Organise the selected content so that business, deliberation, outcomes and follow-up can be located and understood. Structure should clarify the meeting without inventing a more orderly process than occurred.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Use an agenda-aligned record with clear numbered headings and stable item references. Within each item, present relevant context and information, material discussion and reasoning, then the outcome and actions, combining elements where the item is simple. Group related exchanges coherently rather than reproduce transcript order. Preserve material changes of sequence, reconvened discussion, amendments or procedural events where order affects meaning. Cross-refer to related items and previous decisions where useful. Keep the relationship between discussion, decision and follow-up clear without imposing empty subheadings on every item.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI section 5.3, p16, supports sequential numbering; sections 5.3.2 and 5.3.8 support comprehensible item records and identifiable outcomes. Research: S2, pp9 and 12–14, identifies agenda alignment and the relationship between inputs, deliberation, outcome and follow-up. Professional judgement: the internal sequence and stable references are DESS’s chosen expression standard, not a statutory layout or a claim that each discussion followed that sequence.

##### D Professional rationale

A reader normally searches by business item rather than by speaker or elapsed time. Thematic grouping reduces repetition and connects the evidence to its consequence. Chronology remains essential when a conclusion changed, attendance affected authority or a later item amended an earlier outcome. Narrative order must not imply that information was available before a decision if it arrived afterwards.

##### E Scope for customisation

Use numbered narrative, short subheadings or tables where readability benefits. Organisations can retain their own item numbering or separate public and private sections. Local authority reporting or transaction formats may require another structure. Joint discussions can be cross-referenced, but entity-specific decisions and distinct bodies must remain identifiable.

##### F Application of the standard

Agenda alignment is the organising convention, with coherent grouping of related material and explicit treatment of departures where sequence matters. This does not require an inflexible chronological account or empty four-part headings for every item.

#### WS3 Concision clarity and explanation

##### A Purpose

Express all selected information economically and precisely. This dimension controls wording and explanation; MP3 controls whether material belongs in the record and how much substantive coverage it needs.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Write concise, connected sentences that a reasonably informed future reader who was absent can understand. Remove verbal repetition, filler and unnecessary technical detail while retaining the selected reasons, challenge, qualifications and accountability. Define unfamiliar acronyms and explain specialist concepts only as far as necessary for the business. Use precise terms, unambiguous references and appropriate units, dates and amounts. Use short paragraphs or lists where they improve comprehension. Do not impose fixed lengths or compress a material explanation into vague statements such as “discussion followed” or “appropriate assurance was received”.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI sections 3, 5.3 and 5.3.2, pp7–8 and 15–18, require clear, concise and sufficiently contextual records; section 5.3.8, p30, discourages undefined jargon. Research: S2, p12, distinguishes concision from simple brevity. Professional judgement: the future-reader test, paragraph choices and preference for precise units are DESS drafting judgements implementing those principles, rather than mandated language or a plain-language legal test.

##### D Professional rationale

Compression can alter meaning when it removes a condition, merges opposing views or turns a management assertion into a board conclusion. Clarity therefore includes logical relationships: why an issue mattered, how it was addressed and what remained unresolved. Explaining every technical term would also obscure the record; the intended reader is informed, but was not at the meeting.

##### E Scope for customisation

Specialist committees may use more technical terminology, while public-facing records may need more explanation. Bullet points are acceptable for distinct considerations and narrative is useful for connected reasoning. A compact house style remains legitimate if meaning survives. A word cap or preference for polished brevity cannot override MP3, MP5 or MP6.

##### F Application of the standard

The standard provides enough explanation for an informed absent reader without a technical tutorial. Concision must preserve material reasons, qualifications and oversight. The professional substance selected by Minuting Policy takes priority over a preferred length or wording style.

#### WS4 Attribution and reference to participants

##### A Purpose

Express identities and contributions consistently once a policy judgement has established whether identifying the participant is necessary. Naming a person is a substantive choice before it is a matter of house style.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Narrate ordinary deliberation collectively as the board or committee. Identify individuals where necessary for a faithful account, including presenters, action owners, delegated authority, conflicts, withdrawal, abstention, formal dissent, material continuing concerns and relevant specialist advice. Where individual participation itself matters, preserve it. Use full names and roles in the attendance record; thereafter use an unambiguous role or name consistently, preferring roles for ordinary executive reporting and actions where they identify responsibility clearly. Do not routinely attribute every question, use unidentified pronouns or imply unanimous agreement through collective wording.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI section 5.3.3, pp19–20, gives purposeful naming exceptions and recognises sector variation; section 5.3.4 covers dissent. Its examples of personal titles describe contemporary practice, not an obligation. Research: S2, pp5 and 28, identifies the collective-accountability tension. Regulatory context: FCA/PRA expectations can make individual contribution material [S9–S10]. Professional judgement: full names with roles, followed by consistent roles or names, is DESS’s selected convention. MP6 determines when a substantive dissent or concern needs naming.

##### D Professional rationale

Collective narration reflects the body’s role without erasing material individual positions. It cannot establish that every participant agreed. Roles often read clearly and support continuing accountability, while names are essential when the record concerns a particular person’s conflict or dissent. Attribution should convey evidence rather than distribute credit for appearing active in a meeting.

##### E Scope for customisation

Organisations may use surnames, agreed initials, personal titles or more frequent names. Named voting or attribution can be required in charity or public-body contexts; financial-services evidence may warrant fuller identification. Where more than one person shares a role, include a name. Personal preference cannot anonymise a conflict, material dissent or required voting record.

##### F Application of the standard

Use roles for ordinary reporting and actions where unambiguous, names for personally specific positions and events, and collective narration otherwise. This is a consistent convention among acceptable alternatives, subject to the material attribution judgements in MP6 and MP8.

#### WS5 Expression of decisions and actions

##### A Purpose

Make outcomes and follow-up easy to identify and interpret without altering their substantive force. MP4 and MP7 determine what was decided or assigned; this dimension determines how it is written.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Express each outcome explicitly using a verb that accurately reflects its status, such as resolved, approved, agreed, recommended, deferred, rejected, received or noted. Set out formal resolutions distinctly and preserve exact operative wording where required or reliably established as the wording adopted. Present substantive actions clearly with the responsible person or body and agreed timing, retaining all material conditions and limits. Use consistent visual emphasis or a short outcome label where helpful. Do not turn receipt into approval, a recommendation into authorisation, or provisional wording into a final commitment.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI section 5.3.2, pp17–18, provides conventional decision formulations and action particulars; section 5.3.7, p26, recognises mandated transaction wording; section 5.3.8, pp30–31, supports identifiable outcome terms. Sector guidance: CC48 supports exact wording of voted resolutions [S7]. Professional judgement: distinct outcome presentation and the vocabulary set are DESS conventions. There is no general law that boards must use “resolved” and committees must use “agreed”.

##### D Professional rationale

Consistent verbs make the record easier to scan and reduce ambiguity. Their meaning matters more than capitalisation. A committee exercising delegated powers can resolve to approve a matter; a board can agree a follow-up without a formal vote. A draft paraphrase must not be represented as the verbatim resolution. Readability must not strip a condition from the operative sentence.

##### E Scope for customisation

Outcome paragraphs, bold labels and action tables are equally acceptable. Third-party forms or legal advice may require specified wording. Preserve the original effect of dates, limits and conditions even if the organisation changes layout. If a material term or exact resolution text is uncertain, describe only the supported outcome and identify the term needing confirmation.

##### F Application of the standard

Use clear, consistent outcome verbs and restrained visual emphasis where useful. Retain actual conditions and limits. Preserve exact operative wording where required or established, and do not represent paraphrased or uncertain text as an exact adopted resolution.

#### WS6 Document Conventions and Presentation

##### A Purpose

Govern the consistent professional presentation of board and committee minutes. This dimension makes the selected content easy to navigate and read without prescribing the management of successive document versions or formal approval status.

##### B Default Professional Standard

Present the minutes in a clear, professional and consistent format appropriate to board and committee business. Use identifiable document and meeting headings, a coherent heading hierarchy, agenda item numbering and reliable cross-references. Express dates consistently using British date conventions and number pages for ease of reference. Apply consistent terminology, abbreviations, names and roles, document references, typography, spacing and visual emphasis. Keep formatting readable and proportionate to the content, with tables or lists where they improve understanding. Presentation must preserve the meaning, relationships and material qualifications established through Minuting Policy; it must not imply facts or procedural events that the evidence does not support.

##### C Supporting evidence

Professional guidance: CGI section 5.1, pp11–12, supports meeting identification; section 5.3, pp15–16, supports clarity and sequential numbering; section 5.3.8, pp30–31, discusses defined acronyms and helpful visual emphasis. S2, pp12–14, synthesises agenda-aligned structure and consistent expression. These are professional conventions, not universal legal formatting obligations. Professional judgement: the complete presentation standard, including page numbering, dates, heading hierarchy and restrained formatting, is DESS’s convention. CGI’s separate guidance on draft marking and approval is contextual material, not a requirement within WS6.

##### D Professional rationale

Consistent presentation helps a future reader find an item, distinguish its outcome and follow references without interpreting changing conventions. Formatting should serve the record rather than decorate it or create a misleading hierarchy of importance. WS2 governs the logical organisation and sequence of selected content; WS6 governs its consistent document presentation. Dates and headings can identify the meeting without determining the status of any particular version.

##### E Scope for customisation

Organisations may choose different fonts, spacing, heading styles, numbering schemes, date formats and document templates through a Customised Minutes Profile. These can be equally professionally acceptable when they remain clear and consistent. Presentation changes must not conceal material information, break references or change the meaning of decisions and actions. Version management and status administration are matters for the relevant product workflow and are not customisation requirements within this dimension.

##### F Application of the standard

Document presentation is part of professional expression. Management of successive versions, declarations of AI involvement, draft-status markings, chair clearance and formal approval status are outside WS6. This distinction does not prevent the minutes from accurately recording a governance event that occurred during the meeting.

### 5 Consolidated DESS Standard Minutes Profile

The following text reproduces all 15 Default Professional Standards in full. They form one universal profile for immediate use without sector-specific configuration. Read them with the authority and scope distinctions in section 2. Particular organisational and committee requirements are addressed through the Customised Minutes Profile; applicable obligations remain in force.

##### MP1 Meeting and business context

Identify the organisation and board or committee, meeting date, time, venue and means of participation, chair, minute taker, members present, others in attendance and apologies. Record material changes in attendance against the affected business. Identify each substantive item and provide enough background to explain its purpose, stage and relationship to previous decisions. Distinguish each entity’s business and the capacity in which participants acted. Include additional location, timing or procedural context where it matters to authority, participation or applicable requirements. Do not invent missing particulars or repeat background that adds no understanding.

##### MP2 Information and evidential basis

Identify material papers and advice actually received or considered, using a clear title or reference and version or date where necessary. Summarise the evidence, assumptions, limitations and corrections that materially affected deliberation or outcome. Distinguish a presenter’s report or assertion from the body’s own conclusion. Refer to supporting papers without treating all their contents as adopted or reproducing them unnecessarily. Preserve material uncertainty and discrepancies; seek clarification of consequential gaps rather than supply an evidential basis that the meeting did not establish.

##### MP3 Materiality and proportionality

Record every substantive item of business and its disposition, with detail proportionate to its significance. Give fuller treatment to material decisions, difficult judgements, significant risks, competing options, important stakeholder effects, substantive challenge and unusual procedure. Keep routine administrative and noting items brief when their context and outcome are clear. Exclude repetition, incidental exchanges and operational detail without governance value. Retain material exceptions, unresolved concerns and mandatory particulars even where the resulting record is longer. Do not use uniform length, financial thresholds or a decision-only summary as the default measure of quality.

##### MP4 Outcomes and decisions

Record the actual disposition of each substantive item: decision, approval, recommendation, referral, rejection, deferral, receipt or noting, or discussion without a concluded decision. Preserve the scope, material terms, conditions, limits and effective timing of an outcome where established. Distinguish a committee’s exercise of delegated authority from its recommendation to another body. Record material voting results, abstentions and formal dissent where relevant or required. Do not infer approval from silence, a favourable discussion or a paper’s proposed resolution. Where the outcome is materially unclear, identify the need for confirmation rather than manufacture certainty.

##### MP5 Decision reasoning and relevant considerations

For substantive decisions, record the principal reasons and enough context to understand the judgement reached. Include material options, arguments, risks, benefits, assumptions, advice and stakeholder or beneficiary effects actually considered, where they explain the outcome. Preserve significant trade-offs, conditions and unresolved limitations. A routine decision may have an evident rationale needing only a short explanation or clear contextual reference. Do not invent reasons, reconstruct a better decision process, or insert generic assurances that statutory duties or every possible alternative were considered. Where material reasoning is not established, identify the gap for professional review.

##### MP6 Challenge and oversight

Record material questions, concerns and challenges, the significant response or assurance given, and their effect on the outcome or further work. Preserve unresolved issues, requests for further evidence, limitations of assurance and any agreed escalation. Record formal dissent and a request for dissent to be recorded; include the material basis where established. Preserve significant unresolved director concerns even without a final decision or a specific request for attribution. Do not claim that scrutiny was robust, satisfactory or unanimous without an evidential basis, and do not record every exchange merely to demonstrate participation.

##### MP7 Actions and delegation

Record agreed board or committee actions, the required work or deliverable, the responsible person or body, and the deadline or reporting point where agreed. Include material conditions, dependencies and reporting obligations. Record delegations distinctly, identifying the recipient, the authority granted and its scope, limits and conditions as established. Distinguish agreed action from suggestion, request for consideration or an offer of assistance. Do not invent owners, dates or delegated powers. A separate action schedule may supplement the minutes but must not replace the substantive record of the commitment or authority created.

##### MP8 Governance and procedure

Record material declarations of interest, identify the person and nature of the interest sufficiently, and state the actual management of the conflict, including withdrawal, non-participation, authorisation or other measures where applicable. Capture quorum issues, material attendance changes, voting procedure, notice objections, adjournments and other procedural events affecting the business. Record relevant chair confirmations when made, and particulars required by applicable rules. Do not assume quorum, due notice, absence of conflicts or procedural compliance from a standard template. Distinguish discussion held without decision-making authority from a validly taken decision; identify unresolved procedural uncertainty for review.

##### MP9 Boundaries and integrity of the record

Record the meeting that occurred, excluding irrelevant conversation, repetition, gratuitous personal detail and unsupported inference. Preserve material disagreement, adverse information and uncertainty. Do not omit significant business merely because a participant says it is off the record. Keep subsequent developments distinct from the proceedings; any necessary post-meeting note must be exceptional, clearly identified and dated. For potentially privileged advice, ordinarily record that advice was received and the relevant business outcome, with appropriate professional legal judgement governing substantive treatment. Do not assume that the AI can independently determine legal privilege. Include sensitive personal or commercial detail only to the extent needed for a faithful and sufficient account. Do not silently sanitise, retrospectively improve or fabricate the proceedings.

Describe prospective actions from the meeting’s perspective without weakening a firm commitment. Use the active voice where it makes responsibility clearer and the passive voice where the collective outcome is the focus. Preserve uncertainty, qualifications and the distinction between an assertion and an established conclusion. # Part B: Operational AI Logic

## DESS Standard Minutes Profile — Operational AI Logic

Version 1.0  |  9 November 2026

### 1 Executive summary

This specification defines the professional reasoning required to produce an AI-generated first draft of UK board or committee minutes. Its authority is DESS Standard Minutes Profile — Professional Standards, Version 1.0, 9 November 2026. It translates the fifteen standards into operational responsibilities without amending them or prescribing a technical implementation.

The process has five logical stages. Stage 1 establishes what the evidence supports. Stage 2 applies Minuting Policy to select content, determine depth and identify genuine clarification needs. Stage 3 applies Writing Style to produce the written minutes and precise clarification questions. Stage 4 independently examines both outputs once and refers findings for one reconsideration by the responsible producing stage. Stage 5 presents the resulting work as an editable Word document with outstanding questions in anchored Word comments.

Stages 1–3 produce and own the work. Stage 4 checks and advises; it cannot overwrite their judgement, require agreement or start repeated validation. After one consideration of findings, the responsible stages retain or amend their work and the outputs proceed directly to presentation. A difference between two AI assessments does not itself justify a question to the governance professional.

The agenda and at least one account of actual proceedings, comprising a transcript, meeting notes or both, are essential inputs. Papers are optional context and do not prove that their recommendations were discussed or approved. No source has automatic priority in every circumstance. Substantive proceedings must be accounted for before materiality is used to select the formal record.

The first draft must preserve the supported meeting, including principal reasons, material scrutiny, actual outcomes, commitments and procedural events. It must not reconstruct a better meeting, suppress inconvenient facts or fill gaps with plausible terms. Uncertainty is first understood, then assessed for materiality, then expressed as a focused question only where professional clarification is genuinely needed.

The output is conventional, fully editable Microsoft Word minutes. Questions remain outside the substantive minute text and appear as comments on the relevant passage or, where content is missing, the relevant agenda heading. The specification ends at handover for professional review. Subsequent editing, circulation, learning, formal approval and management of the official record are outside its scope.

#### Document navigation

Sections 2–3 define authority, scope and stage responsibilities. Sections 4–8 specify the five stages. Section 9 brings the treatment of uncertainty together. Section 10 maps the fifteen standards to their operational responsibilities. Section 11 defines completion and the authoritative source. Examples are illustrative applications, not additional professional rules.

### 2 Purpose authority and boundaries

#### 2.1 Relationship to the Professional Standards

The Professional Standards establish the required professional outcomes. This specification explains the interpretation, distinctions, boundaries and handovers needed to achieve those outcomes in first-draft production. References such as PS MP4 refer to the named dimension in sections 3–4 of the Professional Standards; its section 5 contains the consolidated authoritative defaults. The complete Professional Standards, including their rationale and qualifications, govern interpretation.

The nine Minuting Policy dimensions determine what belongs in the minutes. The six Writing Style dimensions determine how that selected substance is expressed. A decision about whether dissent is material belongs to Minuting Policy; a decision about a consistent reference to the dissenter belongs to Writing Style. Wording cannot remove substantive dissent or turn a management assertion into a conclusion of the body.

Operational requirements below are subordinate to the Professional Standards. They describe professional behaviour rather than new policy. A genuine conflict must be explicitly identified with the affected standard; it must not be silently resolved by replacing the standard with a newly invented rule. No conflict is identified between the specified five-stage process and the authoritative standards.

#### 2.2 Universal profile and particular requirements

There is one universal DESS Standard Minutes Profile, available without sector-specific configuration. This specification does not require sector selection or activate alternative defaults. Particular organisational or committee requirements belong in the Customised Minutes Profile; its creation and operation are outside this specification. Where established requirements or powers are supplied as context, the account must not contradict them. Neither generic drafting nor a preference establishes compliance with every applicable obligation.

Legal duties, regulatory rules or expectations, professional guidance and DESS judgement retain their distinct authority. The operational process does not insert compliance statements because they would be desirable, assume Model Articles were adopted, or treat a committee as possessing the board’s powers. Applicable obligations cannot be overridden by customisation or convenient wording.

#### 2.3 Scope of the specified work

The process begins with meeting evidence and ends with the first draft and outstanding questions handed to the governance professional in Word. It includes professional interpretation, selection, expression, independent examination and one bounded reconsideration. It does not include the professional’s answers or edits, circulation, subsequent review rounds, feedback learning, submission for approval, approval, access administration, retention or minute-book management.

Recording a procedural event is within scope even where administering its subject is excluded. If the meeting approved earlier minutes or decided a records policy, record the actual event under the normal standards. This does not authorise the drafting process to administer approval or retention. Potential legal privilege remains a content consideration under MP9, with appropriate professional legal judgement over substantive treatment.

#### 2.4 Independence from implementation

Stages are logical responsibilities, not separate calls, agents or services. References to inventories, mappings, conclusions and findings identify information that must be available to the reasoning process; they do not prescribe data structures, storage, programming algorithms or product screens. No model, prompt, API, infrastructure or technical validation mechanism is specified. The development team chooses how to realise these professional behaviours.

### 3 Five stage process and ownership

| Stage | Professional responsibility | Result available to the next stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Understand the Meeting Evidence | Establish the meeting account without deciding minute-worthiness. | Source inventory, agenda framework, complete substantive mapping, reconciled evidence and unresolved factual uncertainties. |
| 2 Apply Minuting Policy | Select content and depth using MP1–MP9; decide genuine clarification needs. | Supported content conclusions, significance and coverage judgements, boundaries and selected clarification requirements. |
| 3 Apply Writing Style | Express selected content using WS1–WS6; formulate questions. | Written first draft and associated specific clarification questions. Stage 3 owns both outputs. |
| 4 Validate the Draft | Independently check both outputs once; report supported findings. | Findings for one consideration by the relevant responsibility in Stages 1–3. Producing stages retain authority. |
| 5 Present the First Draft | Transfer the resulting outputs faithfully into Word and hand over. | Editable minutes with outstanding questions in anchored Word comments. |

#### 3.1 Professional distinctions maintained across stages

Stage 1 establishes an apparent outcome as a matter of evidence. Stage 2 decides what supported outcome belongs in the record and which particulars are material. Stage 3 writes it accurately. A possible decision is not made final by passing through these stages. Each handover must preserve the qualifications that affect the next judgement.

Understanding requires a traceable account of all substantive proceedings, including additional business and discussion ultimately excluded from the minutes. Selection requires reasons for substantive inclusion, exclusion and depth where the choice is consequential. Expression requires a draft that retains that substance. Traceability means the relevant source passages, notes or document references can be identified; it does not require citations or transcript extracts in the formal minutes.

#### 3.2 Bounded response to validation

Stage 4 examines the evidence, selected content, written draft and questions independently of the producing judgement. It reports potential deficiencies with their basis. The appropriate producing responsibility considers findings once; necessary consequences are carried through the producing work in that single reconsideration. The resulting draft and questions go directly to Stage 5, without another Stage 4 pass or negotiation for agreement.

The process can finish with a draft containing genuine gaps, represented through precise questions and appropriately bounded prose. It cannot finish by making those gaps disappear through invention. Presentation is not another opportunity to select content, resolve disputes or improve the draft substantively. Sections 7 and 9 define how a disputed validation finding differs from a real evidential uncertainty.

### 4 Stage 1 Understand the Meeting Evidence

Objective: establish a coherent, sufficiently complete and traceable understanding of what occurred before applying minute-taking materiality. All six components are required logical responsibilities; they need not operate as isolated technical steps.

#### 4.1 Identify the Meeting Materials

Primary professional anchors: PS MP1, MP2 and MP9.

##### Purpose and inputs

Identify what evidence exists and what each source can establish. Inputs are the supplied agenda, transcript or meeting notes, optional papers and any supporting documents or meeting particulars. Establish which meeting, entity and governing body each material concerns, together with author, date, version and agenda reference where available.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Classify material by its evidential role. An agenda describes intended business; a transcript or contemporaneous note accounts for proceedings; a paper usually supplies context or a proposal. A note may also contain a later correction or an anticipated resolution, which must be distinguished from its account of the meeting. Recognise incomplete transcripts, missing pages, competing paper versions, unattributed notes and material apparently belonging to another meeting. The source inventory should describe those limitations rather than confer authority merely from a filename or polished format.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

The agenda and at least one account of actual proceedings are essential. If either is absent, identify the missing essential input; do not manufacture a substitute and present a complete first draft as though the prerequisite were met. Optional papers may be absent without preventing a supported draft. A complete-looking transcript is not necessarily accurate, and a formally titled paper is not proof of discussion. Preserve uncertain meeting association instead of silently attaching an ambiguous document to the meeting.

##### Output and interactions

Output is an intelligible inventory identifying the materials available, their roles and limitations, and missing essential inputs. Make this context available throughout Stage 1 and subsequent judgement. This is an analytical basis, not a required user-facing inventory or separate deliverable. Materiality of source gaps is assessed in Stage 2 after their significance can be understood.

##### Illustration

An agenda includes an acquisition paper, but only an earlier paper version is supplied. Identify that version and its limits. The draft may still be supported by clear proceedings, but the earlier paper cannot silently supply the adopted price or final conditions.

#### 4.2 Understand the Agenda

Primary professional anchors: PS MP1 and WS2.

##### Purpose and inputs

Establish the intended business framework without substituting intention for proceedings. Use the source inventory, the agenda and its reliable references, together with associated papers. Understand item numbers, headings, subitems and any indications that business is for decision, discussion or noting.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify the body and entity responsible for each item, relationships between standing business and new proposals, and evidenced links to earlier decisions. Associate papers by substantive subject and references, distinguishing current supporting material from historical context. Treat an item’s stated purpose as an expectation to test against the actual meeting account. Preserve the published sequence as a navigational framework while allowing for the meeting to depart from it. An agenda heading may cover several distinct outcomes, and a single issue may be reconsidered under later business.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not infer attendance, discussion, approval or deferral from inclusion on the agenda. A heading marked “approval” does not prove that approval occurred. Do not infer the substance of an earlier decision from a short recurring-business label. Ambiguous item numbering or overlapping headings should remain identifiable until the proceedings support an association; Stage 1 should not choose an arbitrary location simply to produce a tidy agenda map.

##### Output and interactions

Output is an agenda framework with item purposes, linked materials and relevant relationships, distinguishing intended business from established proceedings. Component 4 uses it to map the actual account; Stage 2 uses the resulting map to assess what must be recorded. WS2 later organises the narrative around the agenda without implying that the meeting followed the printed order.

##### Illustration

An agenda labels a budget item “for approval”, while the meeting requests a revised forecast and makes no final approval. The agenda explains the business intended. The actual disposition must be derived from the proceedings, with deferral recorded only if the evidence establishes it.

#### 4.3 Understand the Transcript and Meeting Notes

Primary professional anchors: PS MP1, MP2, MP4, MP6 and MP9.

##### Purpose and inputs

Understand the meeting as a meeting before attempting to write its minutes. Receive the available account or accounts of proceedings and their coverage limitations, together with the agenda framework. Read discussion in sufficient context to follow evolving positions, corrections and the difference between a proposal and a conclusion.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify the actual progression, changes of subject, arrivals and departures, interruptions, apparent outcomes and unresolved matters. Recognise speakers and capacities only where supported: a diarisation label is evidence to examine, not an identity guarantee. Interpret shorthand and multilingual passages by their substantive meaning, preserving qualifications and technical meaning. Separate an asserted fact, a forecast, a conditional offer and a collective conclusion. Follow later corrections or withdrawals without erasing what remained material to understanding the eventual outcome.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not exclude a passage because it looks too detailed or irrelevant to the eventual minutes; that judgement belongs in Stage 2. Do not convert an unrecorded interval into evidence that nothing occurred. If language, shorthand, audio-derived text or speaker attribution cannot be reliably understood, preserve the uncertain passage and its context. Avoid treating sarcasm, rhetorical questions or a tentative formulation as literal decisions where the surrounding account does not support that reading.

##### Output and interactions

Output is a coherent understanding of the proceedings, with apparent discussions, outcomes, commitments, participant roles and limitations traceable to sources. Component 4 maps it to business; Component 5 tests it against other accounts. No polished minute or final clarification question is required at this stage. Apparent outcomes remain subject to reconciliation and subsequent policy judgement.

##### Illustration

A speaker says “we could ask Finance for this next month”, followed later by the chair agreeing a report for the next meeting. Preserve the development from suggestion to apparent commitment. Do not treat the first sentence alone as an assigned action or infer an owner solely from the subject.

#### 4.4 Map the Evidence to Agenda Items

Primary professional anchors: PS MP1, MP2, MP3 and WS2.

##### Purpose and inputs

Account for every substantive part of proceedings and relate it to the business framework. Receive the agenda, interpreted account of proceedings and associated supporting materials. Use substantive meaning as well as any reliable item announcements or references.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify meaningful subject boundaries and associate exchanges with the agenda items they actually concern. Recognise out-of-order discussion and reunite later returns to the same subject, retaining material changes of position or decision. Where one exchange affects several items, preserve the relationships and identify the main business association without losing the other consequences. Distinguish evidenced deferral, withdrawal, non-discussion and additional business. Preserve proceedings for which the item association remains uncertain rather than discarding them.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Every substantive part of the proceedings must be accounted for even if Stage 2 later excludes it. Conversely, silence in an incomplete transcript does not establish that an agenda item was not discussed. Do not force unplanned business into an unrelated heading or invent an agenda number. Do not duplicate an outcome across items in a way that suggests two decisions. A topic change inferred from context can support mapping, but a material ambiguous association must remain visible to the next judgement.

##### Output and interactions

Output is a traceable agenda-to-evidence mapping, including cross-item links, additional business, dispositions supported by the account and ambiguous or unaccounted-for coverage. The mapping provides the completeness basis for Stage 2 and Stage 4. WS2 determines the clearest written arrangement later; Stage 1 does not decide the final wording or amount of minute coverage.

##### Illustration

A liquidity issue is discussed under the finance report and revisited during strategy. Associate both exchanges, preserving a later decision to request contingency options. The minutes can use a main account and a cross-reference without presenting the request as two separate actions.

#### 4.5 Reconcile the Sources

Primary professional anchors: PS MP2 and MP9.

##### Purpose and inputs

Establish the best-supported understanding without imposing an absolute source hierarchy. Use the inventory, mapped proceedings, notes, paper versions and reliable contextual particulars. Examine agreements, complementary details, contradictions and omissions in relation to the proposition being assessed.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Assess reliability in context: whether the source records the actual event, covers the relevant passage, identifies the speaker reliably, is contemporaneous or later, and is consistent with the surrounding discussion. Independent corroboration can strengthen a reading; repetition of the same underlying error cannot. Distinguish genuine contradictions from a proposal being revised, a decision being conditioned, or different sources describing different moments. A later correction of a transcription mistake can help establish what happened; a later change of business decision cannot be merged into the earlier proceedings.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Resolve a discrepancy only where the evidence supports the resolution. Never average conflicting amounts, choose the more detailed source automatically or prefer a paper’s clean wording over an unclear but material proceeding. A gap in notes may reflect summarisation rather than absence of an event. Preserve unresolved factual uncertainty with the competing readings and relevant source context. Do not yet decide whether the uncertainty warrants a human question, and do not repeatedly seek certainty beyond the supplied evidence.

##### Output and interactions

Output is a reconciled understanding distinguishing supported meeting information, contextual material and unresolved factual issues. Make the reason for any consequential resolution and its evidence available to later stages. Component 6 consolidates this basis. Stage 2 determines whether the uncertainty affects the formal record sufficiently to require clarification.

##### Illustration

Notes say approval was subject to a legal review; the transcript contains a clear chair summary with the same condition. A paper proposed unconditional approval. The paper supplies the proposal, not contradictory proof of the adopted terms. If notes and proceedings instead conflict over the condition, retain the unresolved difference.

#### 4.6 Establish the Evidence Available for Judgement

Primary professional anchors: PS MP1 to MP9.

##### Purpose and inputs

Provide a coherent evidential basis for professional selection while preserving uncertainty and traceability. Consolidate the reconciled evidence by agenda item, together with source roles, coverage limitations, cross-item relationships and unresolved factual issues. Include the particulars and procedural events needed to understand who participated and in what capacity.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Bring together the business considered, substantive exchanges, apparent outcomes, reasons expressed, challenge and responses, possible commitments and relevant procedure. Keep distinctions explicit: reported information versus the body’s own conclusion; proposed terms versus adopted terms; discussion versus outcome; and meeting events versus later information. For each consequential uncertainty, retain what is known, what remains unclear, the source context and any affected business. Maintain enough context for Stage 2 to assess significance rather than merely receiving an isolated ambiguous phrase.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

This consolidation is not a materiality filter and is not the first draft. Do not discard adverse or repetitive-looking evidence before its significance is understood, collapse distinct entities into one board decision, or turn a missing account into a conclusion that no business occurred. An unresolved issue must not disappear merely because one source has a more convenient narrative. Preservation does not mean every uncertainty will become a clarification requirement.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the evidence available for judgement: complete substantive coverage, linked supporting context, supported conclusions and unresolved factual uncertainties. Stage 2 receives all of these, not just confidently interpreted passages. Internal analysis is not automatically included in the Word document. Its purpose is to enable defensible selection, faithful drafting and the independent evidential checks in Stage 4.

##### Illustration

A clear approval is recorded but the authorised expenditure limit is inconsistent between two accounts. Carry both the supported approval and the disputed limit into Stage 2. Do not drop the decision, select a convenient limit or automatically draft a human question before materiality has been assessed.

### 5 Stage 2 Apply Minuting Policy

Objective: determine what belongs in the record, its substantive depth and the genuinely unresolved matters needing professional clarification. This is judgement about content, not sentence construction. Apply all nine dimensions coherently, rather than treating them as nine independent accounts of the same item.

#### 5.1 MP1 Meeting and Business Context

Authoritative standard: PS MP1.

##### Purpose and inputs

Select the particulars and background needed to identify the meeting and understand its business. Receive the Stage 1 evidence basis, participant roles, attendance changes, agenda mapping and established relationships to earlier business. Use reliable meeting metadata alongside the proceedings, without assuming that a system label proves who attended.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Determine the organisation, board or committee, date, time, venue, participation arrangements, chair, minute taker, members, other attendees and apologies supported by the evidence. Establish enough item background to explain purpose and stage without reciting the pack. Preserve entity and capacity distinctions and significant changes of participation against the affected items. Exact arrival times are necessary where they affect understanding; otherwise an item reference can be sufficient. Connect implementation business to an earlier decision only to the extent the relationship is evidenced.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not infer attendance from the invitation list, assume an apology from absence, or treat one group discussion as a single joint decision of several legal entities. Missing essential identity or participation information is a potential clarification requirement under the cross-cutting test. An uncertain incidental detail does not necessarily justify a question. The profile remains universal and does not depend on identifying a sector before these judgements can be made.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the contextual content selected for the minute, including material attendance and capacity distinctions and any clarification requirement. MP8 addresses the procedural consequences of participation; MP4 addresses the resulting outcomes. WS2 and WS6 express and present the particulars without deciding new facts.

##### Illustration

The same people discuss a proposal for two subsidiaries. Record each body’s evidenced business and capacity separately. Shared attendance does not establish that each subsidiary approved identical terms.

#### 5.2 MP2 Information and Evidential Basis

Authoritative standard: PS MP2.

##### Purpose and inputs

Select the information and qualifications necessary to understand the evidential basis of the business. Receive reconciled papers, advice, reports, presentations, assertions and limitations from Stage 1. Distinguish information available from information actually received or considered.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify the material source documents by useful reference and version where this affects meaning. Select the evidence, assumptions, limitations and corrections that informed deliberation or outcome. Preserve whether management reported a conclusion, an adviser gave an opinion, or the body itself reached a judgement. Use document references to avoid unnecessary reproduction while retaining an intelligible account of outcomes and significant reasoning. Treat express adoption of a specified paper differently from mere receipt; do not extend adoption beyond the scope actually established.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Content in a paper is not proof of consideration or acceptance. Do not fill an unclear discussion with a paper’s ideal rationale or treat a scheduled presentation as delivered. An absent optional paper need not prevent a supported account of the meeting, but an uncertain document identity or material assumption may need clarification. MP2 preserves evidential substance; MP3 determines how much of it is significant to the formal record.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the selected evidential content, its attribution and qualifications, reliable document references and unresolved material particulars. Pass the basis to MP5 for reasons and to MP6 for scrutiny; do not duplicate a report summary in every dimension. Stage 3 must preserve the distinction between reported information and a board conclusion.

##### Illustration

Management reports that a supplier will meet a recovery date; directors request supporting evidence. The record should not say that the board confirmed the supplier’s recovery. The management statement and the board’s evidential concern have different status.

#### 5.3 MP3 Materiality and Proportionality

Authoritative standard: PS MP3.

##### Purpose and inputs

Determine significance and depth across the record, including non-decision oversight. Use the complete mapped evidence, outcomes, considerations, challenge, procedure and uncertainties. Judge an item as a whole and the material elements within it, rather than using speech volume as a proxy.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Assess what affects understanding of the body’s business, judgement, accountability and authority. Significant risk, complexity, novelty, competing options, consequential stakeholder effects, conditions, adverse assurance or unusual procedure can justify fuller coverage. Routine noting and administrative implementation can be concise when the context and disposition are clear. Retain each substantive item’s disposition and preserve mandatory particulars and material exceptions. Apply comparable judgement across items while recognising that different matters can need different detail.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not set word targets or numerical materiality thresholds, infer importance from the length of a paper, or reduce all minutes to resolutions. A minor operational suggestion may be excluded; an unresolved control weakness may matter even without a decision. Treat uncertainties proportionately: inability to identify an incidental speaker may have no impact, while inability to identify an action owner may affect accountability. An exclusion judgement cannot erase a material concern or required fact.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the scope and depth of selected content, including the significant elements that compression must preserve and the judgement on whether uncertainties affect the record. MP2 establishes evidential status; MP3 determines its governance significance. MP9 protects integrity when excluding incidental material. WS3 shortens expression only within the substantive coverage selected here.

##### Illustration

A long market update contains no material discussion and is noted. A short exchange reveals an untested covenant assumption affecting financing approval. The second may need more explanation than the first despite taking much less meeting time.

#### 5.4 MP4 Outcomes and Decisions

Authoritative standard: PS MP4.

##### Purpose and inputs

Determine the actual disposition and supported operative terms of the business. Receive the reconciled progression, chair summaries, any reliable voting record, proposals, conditions and evidenced authority context. Distinguish the body’s conclusion from an individual’s view.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Determine whether the item concluded in approval, agreement, recommendation, referral, rejection, deferral, receipt, noting or discussion without a concluded decision. Preserve material scope, conditions, effective timing and limits. A later express qualification can form part of the final outcome. Identify material votes, abstentions and formal dissent without describing consensus as unanimity unless supported. A committee can exercise delegated powers or recommend to the board; select the description that fits its actual act and established capacity.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not infer approval from silence, a positive discussion, an agenda label or proposed paper wording. Do not invent a vote or disregard an evidenced decision merely because no formal vote is recorded. Where the fact of approval is supported but an important condition is uncertain, retain the supported approval without implying unconditional authority. Where the outcome itself is unclear, do not choose the most plausible one. Select the missing material particulars for clarification under section 5.10.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the supported outcome, its operative particulars, the relevant individual positions and any genuine outcome uncertainty. MP5 supplies the reasons; MP7 distinguishes resulting work and authority; MP8 supplies the procedural facts. WS5 expresses this conclusion faithfully without increasing its force.

##### Illustration

A risk committee endorses a proposal for board consideration but has no established final approval in the account. The minute records its recommendation. A stylistic preference for the word “approved” cannot create the missing authorisation.

#### 5.5 MP5 Decision Reasoning and Relevant Considerations

Authoritative standard: PS MP5.

##### Purpose and inputs

Select the principal explanation for substantive decisions, proportionate to the judgement made. Receive the actual outcome from MP4 together with reconciled deliberation, evidence, options, advice, assumptions and significant trade-offs. Use materiality conclusions from MP3.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify the considerations that actually explain the decision, including risks, benefits, costs, options and stakeholder or beneficiary effects where material. Reasons can be synthesised from a coherent discussion without a single explicit rationale statement, provided the relationship is supported. Preserve significant arguments against the outcome as well as those supporting it. Distinguish a risk accepted with mitigation from a risk treated as absent. Routine implementation may need only a brief explanation or clear reference to the earlier decision.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not add a rationale because it would make the decision more defensible, claim all alternatives were considered, or insert a directors’ duties recital without evidence. Relevant considerations in a paper are not automatically the board’s reasoning. A substantive outcome with no supported principal rationale can need clarification. An obvious routine administrative purpose can be expressed from the evidenced context without generating a ritual question for every approval.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the selected principal reasons, material competing considerations and limitations, or an identified requirement to clarify missing reasoning. MP4 determines what was decided; MP5 explains why. MP6 captures how significant assumptions were tested and whether concerns remained. The same exchange can support both dimensions but should be narrated once coherently.

##### Illustration

Directors prefer a higher-cost option because the shorter transition reduces evidenced service risk. Preserve the cost trade-off and service rationale. Do not substitute a generic statement about maximising long-term value or infer that every other option was analysed.

#### 5.6 MP6 Challenge and Oversight

Authoritative standard: PS MP6.

##### Purpose and inputs

Select meaningful scrutiny, assurance and unresolved concerns, whether or not a resolution followed. Use the reconciled questions, concerns, responses, requests for evidence and individual positions. Consider their significance and their connection to the eventual outcome.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify the substance of the issue tested, the material response or assurance, and what changed or remained unresolved. Distinguish an answer being offered from the body accepting it as sufficient. Preserve requests for further work and escalation. Distinguish an initial objection later addressed from formal dissent at the conclusion or a significant continuing concern. Identify a person where the position is materially individual, while leaving ordinary deliberation collectively narrated. If a question materially exposes uncertainty, preserve that consequence even when the meeting did not resolve it.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not narrate every question, award credit for participation or insert adjectives such as robust to stand in for substantive evidence. Do not imply that management’s response removed a concern unless supported. A known unresolved concern can be recorded as such without automatically asking how it was resolved. Clarification is needed where an important fact about the challenge, response or continuing position remains genuinely unclear, not merely because the oversight identified a problem.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the selected oversight substance, material responses, unresolved positions, dissent and any necessary attribution or clarification requirement. MP5 incorporates reasoning supported by the scrutiny; MP7 captures agreed follow-up; WS4 expresses identities. A dissenting position must not be lost in a collective formulation or transformed into unanimity by neutral prose.

##### Illustration

A director queries the sensitivity of cash forecasts; management cannot provide the stress case and agrees to return with it. Record the concern, limitation and follow-up. Do not describe the board as satisfied simply because a response was given.

#### 5.7 MP7 Actions and Delegation

Authoritative standard: PS MP7.

##### Purpose and inputs

Select substantive follow-up and any grant of authority with its actual boundaries. Receive apparent commitments, the supported outcomes, named or role-based responsibilities, time references, conditions and evidenced authority context. Distinguish a required deliverable from a suggestion or forecast.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify agreed work, its recipient or owner, timing or reporting point, material dependencies and conditions. Select board- or committee-level follow-up with governance value rather than every incidental operational instruction. Distinguish asking someone to develop options from empowering them to choose and implement an option. For delegation, preserve who may act, the decision authorised, its limits, consultation conditions and any obligation to report back. Where several people are involved, retain the responsibility actually established rather than inventing a lead.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not infer agreement from a tentative offer, assign the presenting executive by default, create an artificial deadline or treat a cap as indicative if it was binding. Keep an established action even when a material detail is missing and select that detail for clarification. If the action itself is uncertain, do not turn an intention into a commitment. A separate action schedule cannot substitute for the substantive commitment in the minutes.

##### Output and interactions

Output is each selected action or delegation, with supported terms, responsibility and limits, together with material omissions needing clarification. MP4 establishes the governing outcome; MP7 identifies work and authority created by it. WS5 must retain their force and keep the delegation distinct from administrative implementation.

##### Illustration

The board authorises the finance director to finalise borrowing within a stated cap after consulting the chair. Preserve both limits. “Finance to arrange borrowing” would lose decision authority and its conditions; “Finance to consider borrowing” would weaken the grant.

#### 5.8 MP8 Governance and Procedure

Authoritative standard: PS MP8.

##### Purpose and inputs

Select procedural facts needed to understand participation and the circumstances of decisions. Use reconciled interests, attendance changes, participation restrictions, chair confirmations, notice objections, voting events, interruptions and relevant established governing context.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Identify the person and nature of a declared interest sufficiently and the steps actually taken, including withdrawal, non-participation or an express authorisation where supported. Assess which attendance changes matter for a particular item. Record material quorum issues, challenged notice, connectivity failures, adjournment or other procedure affecting business. Distinguish a chair’s evidenced confirmation from an independent legal conclusion. If approval of earlier minutes is actual business, record that event accurately without administering the approval process.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not insert standard assurances of due notice, quorum, no conflicts or compliance without support. A declared conflict is not proof of valid authorisation; an attendance list is not always sufficient to establish participation throughout. Do not certify legal validity from incomplete constitutional information. Select consequential procedural uncertainty for professional clarification while preserving established facts. Particular requirements may be supplied through organisational context without making sector identification a condition of the universal default.

##### Output and interactions

Output is the procedural content and its connection to attendance, authority and outcomes, with unresolved material facts or professional determinations identified. MP1 records participation context; MP8 addresses procedural significance; MP4 retains the supported outcome without an unsupported validity assurance. WS4 names the relevant individual where identity is necessary.

##### Illustration

A director declares an interest and the notes confirm withdrawal for the relevant item, but the account does not establish whether sufficient eligible members remained. Record the declaration and withdrawal; do not add that the meeting remained quorate. Assess whether the missing fact requires clarification.

#### 5.9 MP9 Boundaries and Integrity of the Record

Authoritative standard: PS MP9.

##### Purpose and inputs

Protect the selected account against invention, misleading exclusion and retrospective alteration. Receive the complete evidence basis and content conclusions across MP1–MP8, including sensitive passages, later information, unresolved concerns and proposed exclusions.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Exclude irrelevant conversation, repetition and gratuitous personal detail where they add no material understanding. Preserve significant business, adverse information and disagreement even after an off-the-record request. Keep later developments separate from the meeting. A correction that establishes what actually occurred differs from a later decision or new information. Any necessary post-meeting note is exceptional, separately identified and dated. For potentially privileged advice, ordinarily select the fact that advice was received and the relevant business outcome; substantive treatment depends on appropriate professional legal judgement.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not use reputational convenience or generic confidentiality as a reason to erase material content. Do not independently conclude privilege from a speaker’s title or a label. Nor should a business outcome be removed merely because legal advice preceded it. Where substantive treatment needs legal judgement, identify that genuine clarification requirement without reproducing the sensitive advice unnecessarily in the question. Do not design permissions, approval, retention or document-status management within this content judgement.

##### Output and interactions

Output is a selected account that preserves the meeting’s material substance, with legitimate exclusions, temporal boundaries and sensitive-content limitations. MP9 constrains every policy dimension and later expression. Stage 3 must not reintroduce excluded detail, hide an adverse fact through euphemism or transform a subsequent update into contemporaneous reasoning.

##### Illustration

A commercial decision followed legal advice and a participant asks for the discussion to be off the record. Retain the supported business decision. Treat the advice carefully and identify any genuine need for legal judgement about substance; neither transcribe it automatically nor erase the decision.

#### 5.10 Decide genuine clarification requirements

Stage 1 supplies unresolved factual uncertainties and their context. Stage 2 considers what each uncertainty would change in the formal record. A clarification requirement arises where information or professional judgement necessary for an accurate, sufficiently complete record remains unresolved. The requirement identifies the issue and what must be established; it is not yet the final question.

Consider whether the available evidence already supports a reliable conclusion, whether the issue affects identity, outcome, scope, reasons, accountability, procedure or material oversight, and whether a faithful record can simply state an established limitation. Minor uncertainty with no effect on substantive accuracy does not warrant a question. Lack of a response in the meeting can itself be the established fact, rather than a reason to ask for a later answer to be backdated.

For each selected requirement, preserve the known facts, the unresolved point, its material consequence, the relevant source context and the agenda item or content to which it relates. Combine overlapping requirements where one answer would resolve the same substantive issue. Keep independent issues distinct. This is professional judgement, not a numerical confidence threshold or a prescribed technical record format.

Stage 2 also establishes the boundary for drafting: which facts can be stated, which terms must remain open, and which descriptions would overstate the evidence. If an approval is certain but its limit is unknown, preserve the supported act without implying a broader authorisation. If the outcome itself is uncertain, describe only what is supported and seek confirmation. Never write a question as though the desired answer has already been established.

#### 5.11 Interactions between the policy dimensions

| Relationship | Division of responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| MP2 and MP3 | MP2 establishes evidential status and significant limitations; MP3 determines the governance significance and amount of coverage. An unreliable fact is not made reliable by being important. |
| MP4 and MP5 | MP4 establishes the actual outcome and terms; MP5 establishes its supported explanation. A sound rationale does not prove that approval occurred. |
| MP5 and MP6 | MP5 selects reasons and trade-offs; MP6 selects scrutiny, responses and unresolved concerns. One exchange may serve both without being repeated. |
| MP4 and MP7 | MP4 establishes the disposition; MP7 distinguishes follow-up work from delegated authority and preserves responsibility and limits. |
| MP9 and all others | Integrity controls omissions, unsupported inference, temporal changes and sensitive substance throughout. It does not replace the other dimensions’ content judgements. |

### 6 Stage 3 Apply Writing Style

Objective: produce two owned outputs from the selected content: professionally written minutes and precise outstanding clarification questions. The six dimensions express policy conclusions; they do not reopen content selection. Word comments are created at Stage 5, after the questions have been formulated and the single validation procedure completed.

#### 6.1 WS1 Voice Tense and Tone

Authoritative standard: PS WS1.

##### Purpose and inputs

Express the selected account in formal, plain British English with objective third-person narration. Receive the selected content, its attribution, degree of certainty, temporal relationships and supported commitments. Preserve the difference between a fact, a reported assertion and a forecast.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Use reported speech and past tense for proceedings, with future actions expressed from the meeting’s perspective. Choose active wording where it clarifies responsibility and passive wording where the collective outcome is central. Retain the force of firm commitments, conditional approvals and tentative statements. Keep tone neutral when describing disagreement, adverse assurance or unsuccessful proposals. Preserve necessary exact operative wording even if its grammar differs from the surrounding narrative.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not convert “would provide” into “should consider providing” where this weakens an agreed action. Do not turn “expected” into “confirmed”, or “management reported” into an established board finding. Avoid unsupported performance language such as comprehensive scrutiny or full assurance. Grammar cannot settle uncertainty selected by Stage 2; carry it faithfully into the bounded prose and associated question.

##### Output and interactions

Output is narrative that preserves agency, timing and certainty. WS5 governs precise outcome and action expression; WS4 governs consistent participant references. If expression exposes an ambiguity, retain the producing responsibility: the substantive clarification judgement is Stage 2’s, not a new writing preference.

##### Illustration

Where the finance director agreed to provide a report by 30 November, express that commitment directly. If management only expected a forecast to improve, retain the forecast as reported expectation rather than a promise or a board conclusion.

#### 6.2 WS2 Structure and Sequence

Authoritative standard: PS WS2.

##### Purpose and inputs

Organise selected content around the agenda while retaining material chronology and relationships. Receive the agenda mapping, selected content, cross-item links, actual progression and any changes in outcome or participation. Use the coverage and depth determined by Stage 2.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Within an item, connect necessary context and information to material deliberation, reasoning, outcome and follow-up. Simple items need not carry separate subheadings for each element. Reunite related exchanges that occurred at different points where this improves understanding, but preserve when later information changed a position or an earlier decision. Use cross-references for shared business and identify additional business clearly without suggesting it was in the published agenda. Keep each entity’s act distinguishable.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not organise by transcript turns or assume chronological consolidation is always harmless. A later forecast cannot be placed before an earlier approval so that it appears to have informed it. Do not duplicate an action under two items or drop a supported non-discussion or deferral conclusion. An ambiguous mapping is not resolved by assigning the material to the most convenient heading.

##### Output and interactions

Output is a coherent narrative order with stable item references, identifiable outcomes and appropriate links. WS2 decides the logical arrangement; WS6 applies consistent document headings and presentation. Stage 2’s selection and temporal boundaries remain authoritative. Questions are associated with the passage or heading affected by the selected uncertainty.

##### Illustration

A proposal is initially deferred, then reconsidered after additional evidence arrives later in the same meeting. The account can be grouped under one item, but must preserve the reconsideration rather than portray an uninterrupted approval based on evidence available from the outset.

#### 6.3 WS3 Concision Clarity and Explanation

Authoritative standard: PS WS3.

##### Purpose and inputs

Convert selected substance into concise, connected prose intelligible to an informed absent reader. Receive the material content, required depth, relationships, technical terms, figures and qualifications. Distinguish verbal repetition from substantive repetition that signals a continuing unresolved issue.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Remove filler, dialogue and duplicated explanations while retaining the principal reasons, challenge, response and accountability selected by Minuting Policy. Make logical links explicit where they explain why an issue mattered or what followed. Define unfamiliar abbreviations and explain specialist concepts only as far as needed. Preserve units, currency, period and comparison basis so figures are not misleading. Use short paragraphs or lists when they clarify distinct considerations rather than fragmenting connected reasoning.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not impose fixed length, eliminate conditions or merge opposing positions to achieve fluent prose. A phrase such as “discussion followed” cannot replace material reasoning. Technical clarity does not authorise a new expert explanation unsupported by the proceedings. Missing evidence must not be concealed by vague language or filled with apparent common knowledge.

##### Output and interactions

Output is concise but substantively sufficient text, retaining all selected meaning and any necessary qualified account. MP3 controls amount of substantive coverage; WS3 controls economy of expression. WS1 preserves tone and certainty, while WS5 keeps operative terms precise. Clarification questions use the same clarity principles without entering the formal narrative.

##### Illustration

A report cites an annual savings estimate subject to implementation costs and an uncertain start date. A concise summary must retain those qualifications where material. “The project would save £2 million” could misstate an estimate even though it is shorter.

#### 6.4 WS4 Attribution and Reference to Participants

Authoritative standard: PS WS4.

##### Purpose and inputs

Express the identities selected as material without recreating a speaker-by-speaker record. Receive participant identities and roles, the attribution decisions under MP1, MP2, MP6–MP8, and any uncertain speaker identification. Ordinary deliberation starts from collective narration.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Use the board or committee for collective consideration. Attribute reported management information and specialist advice so their status remains clear. Identify action owners, delegation recipients, conflicted participants, abstentions, formal dissent and significant individual continuing concerns where required by the content judgement. Use full names and roles for attendance; thereafter prefer an unambiguous role for ordinary executive reporting and actions, or a name where the event or position is personally specific. Maintain references consistently across items.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not make every question personal, imply unanimity through “the board agreed” where dissent must be preserved, or use a role that could refer to several people. Do not infer the dissenter from an unreliable diarisation label. Where identity is materially necessary but uncertain, retain the clarification requirement rather than choose a likely participant or anonymise a significant personal position for convenience.

##### Output and interactions

Output is consistent and purposeful attribution that respects collective responsibility and material individual accountability. Stage 2 decides the need to identify a person; WS4 selects a faithful expression of that decision. WS6 ensures presentation consistency but cannot remove required identification. The corresponding question should name the uncertainty neutrally without assigning a position to an unconfirmed person.

##### Illustration

A director’s formal dissent is established but a speaker label is unreliable. Preserve the substantive dissent and the need to establish identity. A question should ask who requested the recorded dissent, rather than state that a particular director dissented and request confirmation of that assumption.

#### 6.5 WS5 Expression of Decisions and Actions

Authoritative standard: PS WS5.

##### Purpose and inputs

Make operative outcomes and commitments explicit while preserving their exact professional effect. Receive the outcome categories, supported terms, actions, delegations and unresolved particulars selected under MP4 and MP7, together with any reliably established exact resolution text.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Use verbs that distinguish approval, resolution, agreement, recommendation, referral, deferral, rejection, receipt and noting. State conditions within or clearly connected to the outcome. Present material follow-up with the supported owner, deliverable, timing and dependencies. Distinguish work assigned from authority conferred. Set out formal resolutions distinctly, preserving exact operative wording where necessary and supported. Restrained outcome labels or emphasis can aid identification without changing meaning.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not call a paraphrase exact, strengthen a request into an obligation, expand a delegation or change an agreed deadline to a convenient calendar date. If a relative date cannot be established reliably, retain the supported relationship and the relevant clarification requirement. Do not insert a placeholder term as though agreed. Missing particulars must remain external questions while the body records only the supported commitment and its known limits.

##### Output and interactions

Output is clear decision and action wording consistent across the draft. WS1 supplies tense and narrative form; WS2 locates outcomes coherently. MP4 and MP7 retain substantive authority. If wording would suggest unconditional approval despite a known unresolved condition, revise the expression within the established content boundary rather than erasing the decision or inventing the condition.

##### Illustration

“The committee recommended approval to the board” accurately expresses a recommendation. “The committee approved” would change the outcome. Similarly, authority to settle terms up to £500,000 cannot become an unrestricted authority to contract.

#### 6.6 WS6 Document Conventions and Presentation

Authoritative standard: PS WS6 Document Conventions and Presentation.

##### Purpose and inputs

Establish consistent professional document presentation suitable for editable Word minutes. Receive the written narrative structure, meeting particulars, references and identities. Apply one coherent set of presentation conventions without changing the selected facts.

##### Interpretation and professional decision logic

Use identifiable meeting and document headings, a clear hierarchy, agenda item numbering, meaningful cross-references and page numbering. Apply British conventions consistently for dates, times, numbers and language, preserving underlying values and units. Use consistent terminology, abbreviations, names, roles and paper references. Choose readable typography, spacing and restrained emphasis, with tables or lists where useful. Prepare presentation decisions that can be carried into Word without turning the document into an image or a transcript.

##### Decision boundaries and uncertainty

Do not add mandatory AI-use declarations, draft labels on every page, approval status fields or chair-clearance controls as requirements of the Professional Standards. Presentation cannot imply that a procedural event occurred, that a draft is approved, or that a confidentiality label determines privilege. An unknown meeting particular cannot be filled merely to complete a template.

##### Output and interactions

Output is a professionally presented written draft with consistent layout and references, ready for the independent check. WS2 governs the logical narrative; WS6 governs its document conventions. Stage 5 implements the settled presentation in the editable deliverable and anchors existing questions; it does not use formatting as an opportunity to rewrite or decide content.

##### Illustration

An action table can improve readability if the same established owner, deliverable and timing remain clear. An automatically populated signature or approval date would be unsupported administrative content and is not part of this standard.

#### 6.7 Formulate the outstanding questions

Stage 3 receives clarification requirements selected by Stage 2 and owns the final wording of questions alongside the draft. Each question identifies the precise unresolved issue, the information or judgement required and the relevant item or passage. It must be neutral, actionable and answerable in the course of professional review. It must not assume that a proposed answer is correct.

Use the known context to make the question specific without repeating the meeting account at length. Distinguish a request to establish a fact from a request for professional or legal judgement. If a condition is unclear, ask what condition was agreed; do not ask whether the board would prefer a different condition. A request for missing reasoning concerns the rationale of the meeting’s decision, not a new justification the organisation might now wish to adopt.

Consolidate genuinely duplicate issues so one uncertainty does not create multiple comments under different dimensions. Keep separate uncertainties distinct where different information is required. Associate each question with the smallest meaningful affected passage, or the relevant heading if nothing can be written about the missing matter. The association is determined here; Stage 5 creates the Word comment.

Keep all question wording outside the substantive minutes. The narrative must remain faithful to what is supported, without blanks that masquerade as adopted terms or a guessed answer accompanied by a cautious comment. A comment cannot cure a positively misleading assertion in the body. If only part of a decision is established, preserve that part with appropriate limits and ask about the rest.

| Unresolved issue selected by Stage 2 | Suitable question | What the question must avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Action agreed; responsible person not established. | Who was assigned responsibility for submitting the revised forecast requested under this item? | Assuming the finance director was the owner because they presented the report. |
| Approval established; expenditure cap differs between accounts. | What expenditure limit was agreed for this approval? The proceedings and notes contain different amounts. | Choosing one amount in the minute and merely asking if it looks correct. |
| Material dissent established; identity unclear. | Which director requested that their dissent on this decision be recorded? | Naming an unconfirmed dissenter. |
| Potentially privileged substantive advice. | Please confirm the appropriate treatment of the legal-advice discussion in this item, including whether substantive detail should be recorded. | Reproducing sensitive advice in the question or asking the reviewer to endorse an unsupported AI privilege conclusion. |

Output is the complete first draft plus the outstanding questions with their intended associations. If no genuine clarification is needed, produce no questions. Stage 4 checks both outputs; Stage 3 remains responsible for their final expression after the bounded reconsideration.

### 7 Stage 4 Validate the Draft

#### 7.1 Independent examination and its limits

Stage 4 provides one independent professional quality-assurance pass over the draft minutes and clarification questions. It must examine them against the evidence and the Professional Standards rather than accept the producing account at face value. Independence is an evaluative responsibility, not a requirement for another model, agent or technical service.

Inputs include the resulting draft and questions, the meeting sources, their coverage limitations, the agenda mapping and the consequential content judgements needed to understand the work. Examine omitted as well as included business. A check confined to fluent text cannot detect a material omission or an unsupported interpretation inherited from Stage 1.

#### 7.2 Validation responsibilities one to four

##### 1 Factual accuracy

Check names, roles, entity, meeting particulars, amounts, dates, periods, figures and references against relevant evidence. Test the actual status of decisions, conditions, actions, delegations and qualifications, not just isolated words. Distinguish an erroneous statement from a reliable paraphrase. Where evidence conflicts, report the conflict and its effect rather than treating a preferred source as definitive. A factual finding should identify the passage, supporting evidence and potential consequence.

##### 2 Completeness

Compare the draft with the agenda and complete substantive mapping, including additional business and discussion revisited later. Look for omitted material outcomes, reasons, challenges, adverse information, procedural events and commitments. Differentiate omission from legitimate proportionate compression. An unrecorded interval or incomplete account may create a coverage uncertainty, not proof that business occurred or that it was omitted from the minutes.

##### 3 Minuting Policy

Test whether MP1–MP9 have been applied coherently. Check significance, outcome status, reasons, oversight, action and delegation limits, procedure, sensitive-content boundaries and temporal integrity. Do not judge quality by maximum length or by a preferred personal minute style. A policy finding must explain why a different treatment may be required by the professional standard and the evidence, rather than merely recommend a more elaborate account.

##### 4 Writing Style

Check WS1–WS6 for neutral narrative, preserved certainty, coherent agenda structure, sufficient explanation, purposeful attribution, precise outcome wording and consistent presentation. Focus especially on substantive changes introduced through fluent rewriting: omission of a condition, promotion of a reported assertion to a board conclusion, or a weakened commitment. Distinguish a real expression deficiency from an equally acceptable wording preference.

#### 7.3 Validation responsibilities five to seven

##### 5 Document wide consistency

Examine the whole draft for contradictory outcomes, duplicated actions, conflicting owners or dates, inconsistent references, unconnected cross-item relationships and uneven use of names or terms. Consider whether a later passage qualifies an earlier one. A difference may represent real chronology rather than error; report the relationship where the draft makes it unclear. A single underlying issue should not generate multiple inconsistent recommendations.

##### 6 Clarification questions

Check necessity, materiality, factual basis, focus and passage association. Ask whether the evidence already resolves the matter, whether the question leads the reader, whether the body assumes an answer, and whether an important gap has no question. A request to resolve known meeting uncertainty differs from a request to improve the business decision retrospectively. Test whether the intended anchor provides useful context without suggesting a substantive conclusion not supported by the draft.

A recommendation to remove a question should explain why it is unnecessary or answerable from evidence. A recommendation for an additional question should identify the genuine missing information or professional judgement and its consequence. Disagreement with a producing stage does not itself make a question necessary. Stage 4 does not create the final question or place comments in Word.

##### 7 Report findings

Report potential deficiencies to the relevant producing responsibility with the affected passage or omission, the evidential or professional basis, and the change or reconsideration warranted. Make consequential relationships clear so a corrected outcome does not leave its rationale, action or question inconsistent. Describe the issue precisely enough for the responsible stage to consider it without accepting the validator’s interpretation as authoritative.

Route interpretation, mapping or reconciliation issues to Stage 1; inclusion, depth, outcome or clarification-necessity issues to Stage 2; wording, structure, presentation or question-formulation issues to Stage 3. A finding can involve more than one responsibility, but it should remain one intelligible account of the underlying issue. Findings are advisory work information, not an additional required document for the governance professional.

#### 7.4 No independent overwrite

Stage 4 cannot directly alter the substantive draft, impose a preferred judgement, require agreement, or compel a human question simply because its reading differs. Its role is to identify and explain a possible deficiency. Even a confidently stated finding remains subject to consideration by the responsible producing stage, which retains final authority within the Professional Standards.

#### 7.5 One consideration of findings and direct handover

First, Stage 4 independently reviews both outputs once. Second, it reports findings to the responsible earlier stages. Third, those responsibilities consider the findings once, retaining or amending their work for supported reasons. Fourth, the resulting written draft and questions proceed directly to Stage 5. There is no second validation pass, repeated negotiation or requirement for agreement.

When a finding concerns evidence, Stage 1 considers whether its interpretation, mapping or reconciliation should change. If it does, Stage 2 carries the necessary substantive consequences through the selected account and clarification requirements, and Stage 3 reflects those consequences in the minutes and questions. When a finding concerns selection or wording alone, the responsible stages address that issue and its dependent consequences. This is one bounded reconsideration of the producing work, not a restart of the five-stage process.

The responsible stage may accept a finding, accept only the supported part, or retain its original conclusion for a defensible evidential or professional reason. The reason should remain intelligible to the producing process, particularly where it affects an important decision or whether clarification is necessary. No separate justification report is required in the Word output. Retaining a judgement cannot authorise invention or contradict the Professional Standards.

Carry accepted changes consistently into both outputs: removing an unsupported condition may affect the decision sentence, related action and question; resolving an owner may remove the corresponding question. Stage 3 owns the resulting expression and question set. These consequences are part of the single reconsideration, not a new independent validation round. Stage 5 receives the settled producing outputs, rather than choosing between competing versions.

#### 7.6 Disagreement is not an evidential gap

| Situation | Required producing response |
| --- | --- |
| Validator prefers a longer explanation; the original already preserves material reasons. | Retain the original if defensible. Do not ask the human which AI style they prefer. |
| Validator identifies a material condition in overlooked notes. | Consider the evidence once. If supported, carry the condition through the selected outcome, draft and questions; proceed directly to Stage 5. |
| Validator and producer interpret a phrase differently; the wider evidence reliably supports the producer. | Retain the supported interpretation with its reason. Disagreement alone does not become a clarification comment. |
| Consideration reveals a real unresolved difference over delegated authority. | Stage 2 selects the genuine clarification requirement; Stage 3 formulates it and bounds the draft. Proceed without another validation loop. |

The same procedure applies to confidently drafted content and to selected clarification matters. Neither the presence nor removal of a question requires the validator’s agreement. A genuine unresolved evidence gap can reach the professional; an internal difference in assessment does not automatically do so.

### 8 Stage 5 Present the First Draft

#### 8.1 Produce the editable Word document

Stage 5 receives the resulting written minutes, settled presentation conventions and outstanding questions after the single validation and producing reconsideration. Its responsibility is faithful output and handover, not further drafting, policy judgement or quality-assurance review.

Present conventional, professionally formatted, fully editable Microsoft Word minutes. Text, headings, tables, lists and references should support ordinary editing rather than being delivered as page images or a flattened substitute. The document must support normal Word comments and Track Changes. This requires editability; it does not require turning on Track Changes, manufacturing tracked edits or managing subsequent versions.

Carry the content and its relationships into Word without substantive alteration. Preserve conditions, uncertainty, attribution, operative wording and cross-references, and implement the presentation established under WS6. Do not select new facts, remove difficult passages, create an action register as a substitute for the minute, or insert additional administrative declarations. If a transfer would lose a passage or association, preserve the supplied content rather than deciding a new professional treatment.

#### 8.2 Present questions as anchored Word comments

Every outstanding question is presented as a native Microsoft Word comment containing the specific question formulated by Stage 3, including any resulting change from the one reconsideration. It remains outside the formal minute text. A separate question document, inline question paragraph, footnote, endnote or placeholder list is not the standard presentation method.

Anchor the comment to the relevant passage so the professional can read the question with the claim or omission it concerns. Where the information is missing entirely and there is no suitable passage, use the relevant agenda heading or other contextually suitable location already present. Do not invent a substantive sentence merely to provide an anchor. Avoid attaching a narrowly scoped question to the whole document or an unrelated section.

Keep distinct questions intelligible and manageable through normal Word comment functionality. Use the Stage 3 association and wording, without changing the answer sought or expanding the question during presentation. A question about a delegation condition belongs with that delegation; a missing meeting-wide particular can be associated with the meeting heading or particulars. Do not attach commentary explaining internal AI disagreement unless a genuine clarification requirement was selected.

#### 8.3 Hand over for professional review

Deliver the editable Word document, including its contextual clarification comments, to the governance professional. The document is the AI-generated first draft for their review and editing. The professional can respond to questions and prepare later versions during their normal work in Word; those activities are outside this specification.

The handover must preserve both producing outputs. If there are no outstanding questions, deliver the draft without artificial clarification comments or a mandatory nil-return statement. If questions remain, their presence does not authorise unsupported statements in the body or imply that the account is approved. No mandatory AI-use or page-by-page draft declaration is introduced as a supposed professional writing requirement.

#### 8.4 Meaning of presentation completion

Presentation is complete when the resulting minutes are available in an editable Word document and all outstanding questions are available as comments at their intended passages or headings. Normal Word editing, comments and Track Changes must be supported. These are required output characteristics, not a prescription for the technical generation or testing method.

Stage 5 does not repeat evidence reconciliation, materiality selection, writing or independent validation. It does not decide whether the producing stages should have agreed with a validator. It does not seek the professional’s answers or begin another drafting cycle before handover. The five-stage first-draft process ends at delivery of the existing work in the required form.

#### 8.5 Illustrative comment placement

| Question concerns | Appropriate location | Presentation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unconfirmed limit on an established approval. | The decision passage containing the supported approval. | Keep the question in the comment; do not insert a guessed limit in the body. |
| Owner missing from an established action. | The action sentence or action-table entry. | Do not assign an owner to complete the table. |
| A substantive agenda item with no sufficiently clear outcome. | The supported discussion passage, or the agenda heading where no passage can be written. | Do not write an invented decision to create an anchor. |
| Missing meeting time. | The meeting heading or relevant particulars. | Do not populate a presumed start time from the invitation. |
| Legal judgement on sensitive advice. | The relevant item heading or passage acknowledging receipt of advice. | Do not expose the sensitive substance unnecessarily in the comment. |

### 9 Uncertainty and clarification across the stages

#### 9.1 One progression with distinct responsibilities

| Stage | What it does with uncertainty | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Understand | Recognises and preserves unresolved factual uncertainty with evidence and context. | Decide minute-worthiness or formulate final human questions. |
| 2 Judge | Decides which uncertainties materially require information or professional judgement. | Treat every discrepancy as a clarification requirement or write the final question. |
| 3 Write | Produces bounded minutes and neutral actionable questions with intended associations. | Put questions in the formal body or invent an answer to make prose complete. |
| 4 Validate | Checks both outputs once and refers potential deficiencies for one producing consideration. | Force agreement, overwrite the judgement or escalate disagreement automatically. |
| 5 Present | Creates contextual Word comments containing the resulting questions and hands over. | Generate new questions, redraft the substance or begin professional review. |

#### 9.2 Distinguish different kinds of incompleteness

A missing essential input is a prerequisite failure: the absence of an agenda or any account of proceedings must be identified without fabricating a complete first draft. A material factual uncertainty is an unresolved proposition within available evidence. A professional judgement uncertainty concerns the treatment necessary for a sufficient record, such as substantive handling of potentially privileged advice. Minor uncertainty may have no substantive effect. These situations should not all produce the same response.

An unresolved matter in the meeting is not necessarily uncertainty in drafting. It may be perfectly clear that the board received no answer, deferred a decision or requested further evidence. Record that supported fact. A question is needed where the account itself cannot reliably establish a necessary fact or treatment. Do not ask for a substantive answer now simply to make the earlier meeting appear to have resolved its issue.

The process does not require repeated attempts to resolve every uncertainty. A supported conclusion, a bounded account with genuine clarification comments, or identification of missing essential inputs can each be a faithful result in its appropriate circumstances. There is no numerical confidence score, fixed question quota or universal escalation trigger in this professional specification.

#### 9.3 Worked illustration of a genuine clarification need

Illustrative evidence: the agenda includes approval of a facility extension. The proceedings clearly establish approval subject to a limit and consultation with the chair. One account records a £500,000 limit and another a £750,000 limit; the available context does not resolve the difference. The action to finalise the extension is clear, but the delegated limit is not.

Stage 1 maps all relevant exchanges to the facility item, distinguishes the paper’s proposed terms from proceedings, and preserves both conflicting amounts with their source context. It does not select a limit or decide the final question. Stage 2 determines that the limit is material to the outcome and delegated authority. It retains the established approval, consultation condition and action, while identifying the unresolved limit as a clarification requirement.

Stage 3 writes the supported approval and delegation without asserting either disputed amount or implying unlimited authority. It formulates a focused question asking what limit was agreed and associates it with the operative passage. The question remains outside the narrative. The body must make the existence of the limit clear to the extent supported; a comment cannot cure wording that otherwise grants unrestricted authority.

Stage 4 examines the sources, written passage and question once. If it suggests that an additional source resolves the amount, the relevant producing responsibility considers that finding once. If the source is merely the pre-meeting proposal and does not establish adoption, the original uncertainty can defensibly remain. No question is added merely because the validator preferred the proposed amount.

The producing outputs then go directly to Stage 5. It delivers the resulting editable minutes with the specific question anchored as a Word comment to the decision or delegation passage. The governance professional’s later response is outside the process specified here. No second validation or agreement-seeking round takes place.

#### 9.4 Worked illustration where a question is unnecessary

Illustrative evidence: notes abbreviate an executive’s routine update, while the transcript clearly states that the report was received and noted without material discussion. A paper’s draft recommendation suggests approval, but the proceedings do not. Stage 1 distinguishes the proposal from the actual noting outcome; there is no unresolved adoption question merely because the paper used a different verb.

Stage 2 selects a concise noting minute. Stage 3 writes it without an artificial question asking whether the body intended approval. If Stage 4 prefers an approval formulation, the producing stage considers and rejects that suggestion on the evidenced basis. Stage 5 presents the supported noting minute without escalating the disagreement or creating a clarification comment.

### 11 Completion of the operational specification

The specified professional behaviour is a coherent progression from understanding evidence to selecting content, expressing the minutes and questions, independently examining both outputs once, and presenting the result in Word. The required handovers preserve source context, materiality, meaning and unresolved issues without prescribing a technical design.

Coverage comprises six Stage 1 components, all nine Minuting Policy dimensions, all six Writing Style dimensions, seven validation responsibilities and three presentation responsibilities. Stages 1–3 retain production ownership. Stage 4 contributes one independent pass and one opportunity for the producing responsibilities to reconsider; it has no overwrite or agreement-enforcement authority. Stage 5 faithfully presents the resulting outputs and completes the AI first-draft process.

Completion does not require every uncertainty to disappear. It requires that supported conclusions remain faithful, genuine clarification needs are properly selected and expressed, and outstanding questions appear as contextual Word comments. Neither an unanswered meeting issue nor a difference between internal assessments is automatically a question for the professional. Missing essential inputs cannot be replaced by invention.

The specification is ready to guide implementation of the professional behaviour. Choices of models, orchestration, software architecture, prompts, data structures, programming, infrastructure and technical tests remain with the development team. Subsequent human editing, circulation, review, learning and formal record management remain outside this first-draft specification.
